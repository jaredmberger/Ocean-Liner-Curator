# Ship-guide sourcing audit — batch 29 (11 October 2026)

Five distinct guides not previously covered by this recent audit series: `ships/ss-potsdam.html`, `ships/ss-veendam.html`, `ships/ss-nieuw-amsterdam-1906.html`, `ships/rms-queen-mary.html`, and `ships/rms-queen-elizabeth.html`.

This is a selective first-pass review of in-repository guide chronology, evidence descriptions, historical identity, and Key Facts. It is not a comprehensive external cross-check of all historical assertions, and does not certify external URL, metadata, or deployed-image behavior.

| Guide | Finding | Disposition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SS *Potsdam* (1900) | GG Archives reproduces passenger documentation, while MarHisData indexes the successive *Potsdam* / *Stockholm* / *Solglimt* identities. Original tonnage and subsequent careers are differentiated. | Preserve guide. |
| SS *Veendam* (1923) | GG Archives plus Holland America institutional fleet history supply the existing source trail. Wartime German accommodation-ship phase and postwar return separated. The shipyard's Govan location should not be casually “corrected” to Belfast without examining the specific yard register. | Preserve; yard verification is a targeted future research item. |
| SS *Nieuw Amsterdam* (1906) | Existing GG Archives passenger-list links and Holland America fleet context distinguish this ship from the 1938 namesake. The Key Facts contain an expansive “only major transatlantic passenger liner to maintain regular sailings throughout the First World War” claim, qualified as “frequently noted” but not linked to direct comparative contemporary voyage records. | **Open sourcing question:** verify scope, wartime intervals and comparable neutral liners before presenting exclusivity as established fact. No unsupported change in this batch. |
| RMS *Queen Mary* (1936) | Official preservation vessel history and the U.S. National Park Service National Register nomination already supply unusually useful source-level documentation. WWII troopship and commercial identities distinguished. | Preserve guide. |
| RMS *Queen Elizabeth* (1938) | Existing National Records of Scotland, West Dunbartonshire heritage, Hong Kong University and Royal Museums Greenwich records distinguish wartime commissioning, delayed commercial service, and later *Seawise University* fate. | Preserve guide. |

**Decision:** No historical Key Facts or substantive guide content revised. The *Nieuw Amsterdam* exclusivity statement is an explicit source-led follow-up candidate, not a proven error. A clean selective scan should never be treated as verification of all claims.
