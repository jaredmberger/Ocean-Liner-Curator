French Line

Compagnie Générale Transatlantique connected France with New York and a wider Atlantic world for more than a century, from nineteenth-century mail steamers to Île de France, Normandie, and the final transatlantic flagship France.

Founded: 1855 / renamed 1861Core route: Le Havre–New YorkArchive: 19 ship guides

Basic answer

A French transatlantic system

The company began as Compagnie Générale Maritime in 1855 and became Compagnie Générale Transatlantique in 1861. State-supported mail contracts helped define its early purpose. In 1864 the company inaugurated regular Le Havre–New York service, a route that became one of the central threads of French passenger shipping.

That history was never only about prestige flagships. CGT operated mail steamers, migrant carriers, intermediate liners, colonial-route vessels, wartime transports, rebuilt ships, and national showpieces. The famous ships make sense only when seen inside that larger operating system.

Short answer

The French Line was both a transportation network and a national showcase: practical route service on one hand, highly visible design and prestige on the other.

Historical arc

A broad French Line chronology

1855

Compagnie Générale Maritime

The Pereire brothers establish the company that would become CGT.

1861

Compagnie Générale Transatlantique

The company takes the name by which the French Line is best known.

1864

Le Havre–New York

Regular North Atlantic service begins and becomes a defining company route.

1880s–1914

Fleet modernization

Express steamers and larger passenger ships strengthen CGT's competitive Atlantic position.

1920s–1930s

French modernity at sea

Paris, Île de France, and Normandie turn design and national style into major elements of the crossing.

1935

Normandie

The new flagship enters service and captures the Blue Riband.

1950s–1962

Postwar continuity

Liberté and then France carry the French Line identity into the postwar Atlantic.

1974–1976

End of an era

The Le Havre–New York passenger era ends and CGT is absorbed into a new corporate structure.
