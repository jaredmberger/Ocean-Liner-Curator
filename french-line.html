French Line

Compagnie Générale Transatlantique connected France with New York and a wider Atlantic world for more than a century. This hub follows the company from nineteenth-century mail steamers through Île de France, Normandie, and the final transatlantic flagship France.

A French transatlantic system

The company began as Compagnie Générale Maritime in 1855 and became Compagnie Générale Transatlantique in 1861. State-supported mail contracts helped define its early purpose. In 1864 the company inaugurated regular Le Havre–New York service, a route that became one of the central threads of French passenger shipping.

That history was never only about prestige flagships. CGT operated mail steamers, migrant carriers, intermediate liners, colonial-route vessels, wartime transports, rebuilt ships, and national showpieces. The famous ships make sense only when seen inside that larger operating system.
