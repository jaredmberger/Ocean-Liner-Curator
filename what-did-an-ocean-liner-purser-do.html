What Did an Ocean Liner Purser Do?

The purser's department handled much of the ship's administrative life: passenger accounts, valuables, documents, messages, lists, currency, inquiries, and coordination between passengers and the company.

Department: purser's officeWork: records, money, passengersRole: administrative bridge

Basic answer

The purser was part banker, clerk, registrar, and problem-solver

The purser's office sat at the intersection of passenger service and ship administration. Duties varied widely, but could include handling passenger accounts, safeguarding valuables, managing currency exchange, maintaining lists and records, distributing messages, resolving cabin or ticket questions, and coordinating with shore agents and authorities.

Short answer

If a passenger problem involved money, paperwork, identity, messages, or the company rather than food or machinery, the purser's department was often involved.

Paperwork afloat

A liner carried an office across the ocean

Passenger records

Lists, tickets, cabin assignments, account information, and voyage documentation had to remain accurate as plans changed.

Money and valuables

Passengers could need safekeeping, exchange, payments, or account settlement during the voyage.

Messages

The purser's office worked alongside wireless and shore systems to route information to and from passengers.

Arrival formalities

Documentation for customs, immigration, agents, and the company formed part of the administrative handoff at port.

Social position

The purser's department was highly visible

Unlike many technical crew, pursers and assistant pursers worked directly with passengers and often represented the company in moments when travelers needed authority, information, or discretion. On large liners, the department could be sizable and highly specialized.
