SS Mongolia

Pacific Mail Steamship Company · entered service 1904 · Ship Guide

Overview

SS Mongolia was the close Pacific Mail running mate of Manchuria, built by New York Shipbuilding at Camden and entering service in 1904. Like her sister, she connected the American West Coast with Hawaii and East Asian ports in a mixed passenger, mail and cargo system.

Her career later moved far beyond the Pacific. She transferred into Atlantic and intercoastal work, served as USS Mongolia during the First World War, became President Fillmore in 1929, and later Panamanian.

Pacific Service

Mongolia's early route connected San Francisco with Pacific and East Asian ports. Her passenger mix and cargo role show a commercial system shaped by migration, imperial administration, trade and mail rather than North Atlantic prestige competition.

From Pacific to Atlantic

When Pacific Mail withdrew from parts of its Far Eastern passenger network, Mongolia moved into other American-controlled services. NHHC explicitly notes that the ship later operated on both intercoastal and trans-Atlantic routes.

USS Mongolia

Naval History documents Mongolia as USS Mongolia (ID-1615) in 1918–1919. Surviving wartime photographs should therefore be treated as naval-transport material rather than Pacific Mail passenger imagery.

Later Identities

The names President Fillmore and Panamanian belong to later commercial phases. NHHC's ship record specifically confirms both names and the 1946 breakup at Shanghai.

Interpretive Notes

Mongolia and Manchuria are visually and operationally related, but individual attribution should still rely on printed name, date or route wherever possible.
