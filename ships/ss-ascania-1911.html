SS Ascania (1911)

Cunard Line · entered service 1911 · Ship Guide

Overview

SS Ascania was built for Canadian passenger service but began with a different identity on the drawing board. Swan Hunter constructed her for Thomson Line as Gerona; Cunard acquired the ship before completion and renamed her Ascania.

She entered London–Southampton–Quebec–Montreal service in May 1911, with winter route adjustments when the St. Lawrence closed. During the First World War she carried troops eastbound while maintaining the practical transport character for which she had been designed.

Gerona Becomes Ascania

The pre-completion purchase is a real provenance boundary. Builder and Thomson Line records may use Gerona; Cunard passenger material belongs to Ascania.

Canadian Migration Service

GG Archives places the ship on the London–Southampton–Quebec–Montreal route, with a large steerage complement. Winter closure of the St. Lawrence changed port patterns, so exact sailing documents are stronger evidence than a single simplified route label.

1918 Grounding

The Shipwreck Preservation Society of Newfoundland & Labrador records the grounding near Petites, east of Cape Ray. The keel was broken, the ship could not be refloated, and all hands survived.

Interpretive Notes

This is the 1911 Ascania, not Cunard's later 1925 ship of the same name. Build year is essential in any catalog record.
