RMS Caronia (1904)

Cunard Line · entered service 1905 · Ship Guide

Overview

RMS Caronia was the reciprocating-engine counterpart to turbine-powered Carmania. John Brown & Co. launched her at Clydebank on 13 July 1904, and she entered Liverpool–New York service on 25 February 1905.

The near-sisters gave Cunard an unusually direct real-world machinery comparison. Caronia's quadruple-expansion engines were mature technology; Carmania's turbines pointed toward the propulsion system Cunard would adopt for its new express giants.

The Reciprocating Control Ship

Caronia and Carmania were not exact sisters internally. Their machinery difference is the point: Cunard could compare fuel economy, speed and maintenance under closely matched commercial conditions. The turbine result helped shape the company's next generation.

Cruising and Ice Warning

A successful Mediterranean cruise from New York in 1906 helped establish a secondary cruising role. On 14 April 1912 Caronia also transmitted an ice warning reporting bergs, growlers and field ice to Titanic; this belongs to the documentary record of that voyage but should not overwhelm the ship's own history.

War Service

Commissioned as an armed merchant cruiser in August 1914, Caronia patrolled Atlantic approaches and waters near New York before later troopship employment. Postwar she returned to civilian work and changed routes and class arrangements several times.

Taiseiyo Maru

After Cunard disposal, Japanese owners renamed the ship Taiseiyo Maru. The name belongs to the final delivery-to-breakers phase and should not be applied to Cunard-era artifacts.

Interpretive Notes

This 1904-launched Caronia must be distinguished from Cunard's famous 1947 green-hulled Caronia. Build year is the safest shorthand.
