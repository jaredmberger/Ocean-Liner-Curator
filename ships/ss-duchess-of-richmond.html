SS Duchess of Richmond

Canadian Pacific Steamships · entered service 1929 · Ship Guide

Overview

SS Duchess of Richmond was a Canadian Pacific Duchess-class liner built by John Brown & Co. at Clydebank as yard no. 523. She entered service in March 1929 and, like her sisters, worked the seasonal Britain–Canada system while also participating in the growing cruise trade.

She survived extensive Second World War troopship service and was rebuilt afterward as Empress of Canada. That second identity lasted until a major fire at Liverpool in January 1953 ended her career.

The Duchess Pattern

Duchess of Richmond shared the standard two-funnel profile, roughly 20,000-ton scale and 18-knot service envelope of the class. Passenger lists preserved by GG Archives show her in cabin and tourist service on Southampton–Cherbourg–Quebec–Montreal routes during the 1930s.

War and Postwar Rebirth

Wartime requisition shifted the ship from Canadian Pacific passenger culture into troop transport. She survived the conflict and was selected for a major postwar rebuild rather than disposal.

In 1947 she emerged as Empress of Canada. This was not the same ship as Canadian Pacific's earlier 1922 Empress of Canada, which had been sunk in 1943. The reused name makes date and prior identity essential in any catalog record.

The 1953 Liverpool Fire

On 25 January 1953, while at Liverpool, Empress of Canada was devastated by fire and capsized at her berth. The wreck was later raised, but the damage ended any prospect of return to service; she was ultimately broken up in Italy.

Interpretive Notes

For this hull there are two valid headline identities—Duchess of Richmond and postwar Empress of Canada. The latter must also be distinguished from the 1922 and 1960 Canadian Pacific ships of the same name.
