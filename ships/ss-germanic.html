SS Germanic

White Star Line · entered service 1875 · Ship Guide

Overview

SS Germanic was the sister ship of Britannic and one of White Star's most remarkably long-lived early liners. Harland & Wolff launched her on 15 July 1874, and she entered Liverpool–New York service on 20 May 1875.

Her history extends far beyond White Star. After a major 1895 rebuild and later service with American and Dominion interests, she became Ottawa and then entered Ottoman ownership as Gül Djemal, later Gülcemal, surviving until 1950.

Record Ship and Rebuilt Veteran

Norway-Heritage records the 1876 westbound crossing at 7 days, 15 hours, 37 minutes. Nearly two decades later, the 1895 reconstruction replaced the original machinery and materially changed the ship, creating another useful photographic dating boundary.

Ottawa

After White Star and a brief American Line charter, the ship was sold to Dominion Line in 1905 and renamed Ottawa for Canadian service. That name marks a new route and company identity.

Gül Djemal / Gülcemal

Sold to Ottoman owners in 1911, the former Germanic entered an extraordinarily long third life as Gül Djemal. She survived a 1915 torpedo attack, later resumed service, and remained in Turkish use for decades.

Interpretive Notes

This hull generated authentic material under at least three major identities. White Star Germanic, Dominion Ottawa and Ottoman/Turkish Gül Djemal/Gülcemal should never be flattened into one catalog period.
