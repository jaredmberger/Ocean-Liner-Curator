SS Lafayette (1915)

Compagnie Générale Transatlantique · entered service 1915 · Ship Guide

Overview

This page covers the 1914-built ship completed as SS Lafayette in 1915, not CGT's later 1930 motor liner of the same name. Launched at Port-de-Bouc on 27 May 1914 as Île de Cuba, she had been intended for the Antilles, Central America and Mexico services. Wartime conditions redirected the unfinished ship to Bordeaux–New York service, and she entered service as Lafayette in November 1915.

The distinction is crucial because the previous page metadata had accidentally described the 1930 ship while the body correctly followed the 1914 hull. The revised guide aligns title, metadata and narrative around one vessel: Île de Cuba → Lafayette → Mexique.

A Wartime Redirection

The ship's original name and intended route make her a useful example of how war could alter liner planning before a vessel even entered commercial service. Rather than begin the Caribbean and Mexico career for which Île de Cuba had been ordered, CGT redirected the completed vessel to the wartime Bordeaux–New York route and renamed her Lafayette.

This is more than a naming footnote: artifacts marked Île de Cuba, Lafayette and Mexique document different operational identities even though they relate to the same hull.

Hospital Ship and Troop Transport

French naval and bibliographic records document Lafayette as a hospital ship during the latter First World War period and as a troop transport during the transition back toward peace. The Bibliothèque nationale de France specifically records requisition from 1916 to 1919 for troop-transport and hospital-ship service.

Wartime photographs or documents should therefore be read through military context, not retroactively described as ordinary French Line passenger material.

Mexique

In 1928 CGT rebuilt and renamed the vessel Mexique, returning the hull toward the Central American and Mexican service originally envisioned before the First World War. That phase deserves its own identity in catalogs rather than being described merely as “late Lafayette.”

Requisitioned again in 1939, Mexique served as an auxiliary cruiser and struck a mine near Le Verdon on 19 June 1940.

Interpretive Notes

CGT used the name Lafayette on multiple ships. The 1930 motor liner was a different vessel entirely, and Normandie was later renamed USS Lafayette by the United States. Date and hull history are therefore essential whenever “Lafayette” appears without further context.
