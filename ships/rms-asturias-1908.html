RMS Asturias (1908)

Royal Mail Steam Packet Company · entered service 1908 · Ship Guide

Overview

RMS Asturias was a Royal Mail Steam Packet Company liner built by Harland & Wolff at Belfast for Southampton–South America service. Launched on 26 September 1907 and delivered in January 1908, she belonged to Royal Mail's “A-series” of passenger liners serving the River Plate trade.

During the First World War she became the hospital ship HMHS Asturias. Torpedoed by UC-66 on 20 March 1917, she was beached on the South Devon coast and later salvaged. After the war the hull returned to commercial use under the new name Arcadian.

Royal Mail's South Atlantic System

Asturias linked Britain with Brazil and the River Plate in a service where mails, migrants, cargo and first-class travel all mattered. Her running mates included Amazon, Aragon, Araguaya and Avon.

Hospital Ship

Converted after the outbreak of war, HMHS Asturias carried wounded personnel to Britain. On 20 March 1917 UC-66 torpedoed the hospital ship off the South Devon coast. She was run aground to prevent sinking and later salvaged.

Arcadian

After periods of salvage and static use, Royal Mail repurchased and rebuilt the vessel. In 1923 she emerged as Arcadian for cruising, a major change in commercial identity and onboard use.

Interpretive Notes

The provenance chain is RMS Asturias → HMHS Asturias → salvaged/hulk phase → Arcadian. The later 1925-built Asturias is a different ship entirely.
