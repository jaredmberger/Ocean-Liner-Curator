MS Stockholm (1948)

Swedish America Line · entered service 1948 · Ship Guide

Overview

MS Stockholm was Swedish America Line's compact postwar diesel liner, launched by Götaverken at Gothenburg on 9 September 1946 and entering Gothenburg–New York service in February 1948. Smaller and more austere than the line's earlier prestige ships, she reflected a postwar emphasis on economical, dependable transatlantic transport.

Her fame was transformed on 25 July 1956, when she collided with the Italian liner Andrea Doria in fog off Nantucket. Stockholm survived with severe bow damage and participated in the rescue effort. The same hull then continued for nearly seven more decades under a succession of owners and names.

Postwar Swedish Atlantic Service

The new Stockholm was deliberately modest compared with the large prewar liners. Her small size, diesel machinery and relatively limited passenger capacity made her a different kind of North Atlantic proposition: practical rather than monumental.

Andrea Doria Collision

On the night of 25 July 1956, Stockholm and Andrea Doria collided in fog south of Nantucket. The Swedish liner's reinforced bow penetrated the Italian ship's starboard side. Although badly damaged, Stockholm remained afloat and took aboard survivors before returning to New York.

Völkerfreundschaft and the Long Afterlife

Sold out of Swedish service in 1960, the ship became the East German Völkerfreundschaft. Later owners repeatedly rebuilt and renamed the vessel, eventually transforming her almost beyond visual recognition into the cruise ship Astoria.

End of the Hull

After years laid up, the former Stockholm was sold for recycling in 2025 and towed from Rotterdam to Ghent in early July. That closed an operating and physical life stretching from the immediate postwar era into the twenty-first century.

Interpretive Notes

The ship's many later names belong to distinct ownership and cultural contexts. The 1948 Swedish liner, 1960s East German cruise ship and later reconstructed cruise vessel should not be treated as one unchanged identity simply because the underlying hull remained continuous.
