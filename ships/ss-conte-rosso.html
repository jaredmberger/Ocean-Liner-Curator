SS Conte Rosso

Lloyd Sabaudo · entered service 1922 · Ship Guide

Overview

SS Conte Rosso was a large Italian liner built in Scotland for Lloyd Sabaudo. Launched at William Beardmore & Company’s Dalmuir yard in 1921 and completed in 1922, she belonged to the generation that expanded Italy’s interwar passenger presence before the better-known state-consolidated Italian Line era.

Her story has two sharply different halves: a civilian liner career shaped by corporate reorganization and changing routes, followed by requisition as a troop transport in the Second World War. She was sunk by the British submarine HMS Upholder on 24 May 1941 with exceptionally heavy loss of life.

Civilian Service and Consolidation

Conte Rosso entered service during a period of intense competition among Italian passenger companies. Early material should be read in Lloyd Sabaudo context; later records reflect the state-directed mergers that brought fleets under Italia Flotte Riunite and then redistributed ships again. By the mid-1930s Conte Rosso was associated with Lloyd Triestino.

This matters for attribution because “Italian Line” can be used too loosely in later collecting descriptions. A dated document, operator imprint, route, and registry are better evidence than a retrospective umbrella label.

Interwar Route and Corporate Context

During the 1920s, Conte Rosso worked within the broad Italian passenger system linking Mediterranean ports with overseas destinations. Her career was shaped less by one permanent route than by the changing commercial priorities of Lloyd Sabaudo and, later, the state-directed consolidations of the Italian shipping industry. That broader network context is important because later summaries often flatten several corporate phases into the single label “Italian Line.”

Her construction in Scotland also places her within a wider pattern of Mediterranean companies commissioning major passenger ships from British yards. The result was an Italian-operated liner whose physical origins, corporate identity, and route history crossed national boundaries well before wartime requisition changed her role entirely.

Wartime Troopship

During the Second World War the liner was requisitioned for troop transport. On 24 May 1941 she sailed in a heavily escorted convoy carrying men toward Libya. Off southeastern Sicily, HMS Upholder, commanded by Lieutenant Commander Malcolm David Wanklyn, attacked the convoy and torpedoed Conte Rosso. The liner sank rapidly.

Contemporary and later official-derived counts vary in detail, but modern Italian research commonly gives roughly 2,729 people aboard, about 1,432 survivors, and 1,297 dead or missing. Because casualty totals have been revised across sources, exact numbers should be attributed rather than presented as if every record agrees.

Why the Loss Matters

The sinking was one of the deadliest Italian maritime losses of the war before the 1943 armistice. Treating Conte Rosso merely as an interwar “Conte” liner understates the scale of her troopship history; treating her only as a wartime casualty erases nearly two decades of civilian service. Both phases belong in the same biography, but should remain clearly separated.
