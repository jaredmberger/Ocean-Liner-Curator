RMS Majestic (1889)

White Star Line · entered service 1890 · Ship Guide

Overview

RMS Majestic was the sister of Teutonic and one of White Star's decisive steps into the modern twin-screw express-liner era. Built by Harland & Wolff, launched on 29 June 1889 and entering service on 2 April 1890, she was substantially larger and faster than the line's 1870s generation.

Her career included a celebrated 1891 westbound record, Boer War transport duty, a major 1902–03 rebuild, and a brief return from reserve after the loss of Titanic.

The Twin-Screw Era

Majestic and Teutonic mark a major break from White Star's earlier single-screw iron liners. Steel construction, twin screws and higher sustained speed made them genuine express ships in the late-Victorian Atlantic competition.

1891 Westbound Record

Norway-Heritage gives the celebrated crossing as 5 days, 18 hours, 8 minutes at roughly 20.1 knots. This is best described as a specific westbound record rather than a generic “Blue Riband” identity.

Long Service and Return from Reserve

Majestic served as a Boer War transport, was heavily refitted in 1902–03, and entered reserve in 1911. After Titanic was lost in April 1912, the older liner returned temporarily to active White Star service before final withdrawal.

Interpretive Notes

This is the 1889-launched White Star ship, not the much larger ex-Bismarck that joined White Star as Majestic in 1922. Date and silhouette are essential disambiguators.
