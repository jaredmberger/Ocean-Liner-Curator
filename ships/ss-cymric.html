SS Cymric

White Star Line · entered service 1898 · Ship Guide

Overview

SS Cymric was a large White Star passenger-cargo liner built by Harland & Wolff and launched on 12 October 1897. She entered Liverpool–New York service in February 1898 and later shifted to Liverpool–Boston.

Her design captures White Star's late-Victorian preference for capacity and comfort rather than record speed. Originally conceived with large livestock space, the ship was altered toward greater passenger accommodation before completion.

Capacity Over Speed

Cymric was not an express liner, and that is precisely why she matters. Her large cargo volume and emigrant capacity show the economics behind White Star's broader Atlantic system.

Boston Years

GG Archives preserves White Star passenger lists for Cymric on Liverpool–Boston in 1906 and again in later years. Route context therefore matters when cataloging ephemera from her long career.

Final Voyage

uboat.net records Cymric among the largest Allied ships hit by U-boats in World War I: U-20 under Walther Schwieger torpedoed her on 8 May 1916. The ship remained afloat for hours before sinking the next day.

Interpretive Notes

Accommodation changed during the ship's career, including a later second/third-class arrangement. Passenger-class claims should be tied to date rather than treated as static specifications.
