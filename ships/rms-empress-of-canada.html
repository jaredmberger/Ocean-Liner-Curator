RMS Empress of Canada (1922)

Canadian Pacific Steamships · entered service 1922 · Ship Guide

Overview

RMS Empress of Canada was a major Canadian Pacific turbine liner built by Fairfield Shipbuilding & Engineering Co. at Govan as yard no. 528. Completed in 1922, she began on the Atlantic/St. Lawrence route before becoming strongly associated with Canadian Pacific's trans-Pacific network and later with long-distance cruising.

During the Second World War she became a troop transport. On 14 March 1943, while in the South Atlantic with troops, personnel and Italian prisoners of war aboard, she was sunk by the Italian submarine Leonardo da Vinci. The casualty figures are reported inconsistently in later sources and should be presented with that uncertainty intact.

Atlantic Introduction, Pacific Identity

The ship's first voyage was Liverpool–Quebec–Montreal, but her public identity soon became strongly trans-Pacific. Canadian Pacific's combined rail-and-steamship system made Vancouver a gateway to Japan, China and other Asian ports, and the “Empress” name carried particular weight in that network.

That dual-ocean career makes route evidence especially important. A Canadian Pacific artifact with the correct ship name may still belong to very different commercial contexts depending on date and itinerary.

Cruising and Interwar Adaptation

Like other large liners of the 1920s, Empress of Canada increasingly participated in cruise work as passenger markets changed. Cruise brochures should be cataloged separately from scheduled liner material because they document a different product even when the ship was unchanged.

1943 Loss and Conflicting Totals

Late on 13 March 1943 the Italian submarine Leonardo da Vinci attacked the unescorted liner in the South Atlantic. A final torpedo struck after midnight on 14 March and the ship sank shortly afterward.

uboat.net explicitly notes disagreement among sources over the number aboard and the number rescued. Its structured loss table gives 1,885 aboard, 392 dead and 1,497 survivors, while the accompanying narrative cites alternative totals including 1,844 aboard and 1,519 survivors. The ship carried roughly 499 Italian prisoners of war, who formed a large proportion of the dead. This guide therefore preserves the disagreement instead of manufacturing a single false-precision total.

Name Reuse

This is the 1922 Empress of Canada. Canadian Pacific reused the name after the war for the rebuilt former Duchess of Richmond, and again for a new 1960 liner. Date or prior identity should always accompany ambiguous catalog records.
