SS Berlin (1909)

Norddeutscher Lloyd · entered service 1909 · Ship Guide

Overview

SS Berlin was a substantial Norddeutscher Lloyd passenger liner built by AG Weser at Bremen and entering service in 1909. Unlike the line's four-funnel express ships, she was designed for high-capacity practical service and carried a very large third-class complement.

The earlier guide incorrectly placed her among German liners seized by the United States in 1917. In fact, this Berlin was ceded to Britain after the First World War and entered White Star Line service as Arabic.

A Practical NDL Liner

Berlin's enormous third-class capacity places migration at the center of her commercial history. She belonged to Norddeutscher Lloyd's broad transportation system rather than the small subset of ships built for Blue Riband prestige.

Mediterranean Service

GG Archives' NDL fleet summary identifies Berlin primarily with Mediterranean–New York service. That matters because a generic Bremen–New York label can erase a major part of the ship's actual route history.

War and Reparation

World War I ended the German commercial phase. The ship was ultimately ceded to Britain under the postwar settlement and transferred to White Star Line, which renamed her Arabic. This is a hard provenance boundary: NDL Berlin and White Star Arabic are one hull but different company identities.

Interpretive Notes

“Berlin” was repeatedly reused by Norddeutscher Lloyd. This is the 1908-built vessel, distinct from the 1868 and 1925 ships. Date, operator and route are essential disambiguators.
