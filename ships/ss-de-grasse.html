SS De Grasse

Compagnie Générale Transatlantique · entered service 1924 · Ship Guide

Overview

SS De Grasse was a medium-sized French Line transatlantic liner built not in France but by Cammell Laird at Birkenhead. Laid down in 1920, launched on 23 February 1924, and entering Le Havre–New York service on 21 August 1924, she served as a dependable running mate beneath the scale of the line's major flagships.

Her long career became much more complex after 1940. German forces took control of the ship, used her as an accommodation vessel, and she was sunk at Bordeaux during the retreat in 1944. Refloated and rebuilt after the war, she returned to French service before later careers as Canadian Pacific's Empress of Australia and, finally, Venezuela.

A French Liner Built in Britain

De Grasse complicates the simple idea that French national prestige meant exclusively French construction. Ordered during the postwar rebuilding period, the ship was built by Cammell Laird and originally associated with the projected name Suffren before completion as De Grasse.

In service she complemented larger CGT ships rather than replacing them. That makes her useful for understanding how a prestigious line actually worked day to day: flagship identity depended on a broader fleet of commercially flexible vessels.

War, Sinking and Reconstruction

After the fall of France, German authorities used the ship as accommodation. During the 1944 retreat she was sunk at Bordeaux. Postwar salvage and rebuilding changed her appearance substantially, including replacement of the original two-funnel profile with a single funnel.

This creates a particularly strong photographic breakpoint: prewar two-funnel De Grasse, postwar single-funnel De Grasse, and then the later Canadian Pacific and Italian identities should be cataloged as distinct phases.

Empress of Australia and Venezuela

Canadian Pacific acquired the ship in 1953 and renamed her Empress of Australia. In 1956 she passed to Sicula Oceanica and became Venezuela, receiving further rebuilding for Italy–West Indies–Venezuela service.

The vessel grounded off Cannes on 16 March 1962 and was subsequently taken to La Spezia for demolition. Her 38-year history therefore spans French, German wartime, Canadian and Italian operational contexts.

Interpretive Notes

The same hull can appear under three major passenger names and two very different De Grasse silhouettes. Name, date, funnel arrangement and operator markings should all be retained in artifact descriptions rather than normalized into a single undifferentiated identity.
