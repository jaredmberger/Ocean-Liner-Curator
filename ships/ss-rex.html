SS Rex

Italian Line · entered service 1932 · Ship Guide

Overview

SS Rex was the larger of Italy's two headline express liners placed into service in 1932. Ordered by Navigazione Generale Italiana and completed after the creation of Italia Flotte Riunite, she was built by Ansaldo at Sestri Ponente as hull no. 296 and combined enormous machinery power with a distinctly Italian program of national prestige.

Her reputation is inseparable from the westbound Blue Riband won in August 1933, but her first voyage had been anything but triumphant: electrical trouble forced a prolonged stop at Gibraltar. That contrast—technical embarrassment followed by genuine record performance—is more revealing than a simple prestige narrative.

A Difficult Debut

Rex left Genoa on 27 September 1932. Near Gibraltar, trouble with the turbo-generators delayed the ship while replacement parts were brought from Italy aboard Vulcania. She finally reached New York on 7 October, more than two days behind schedule.

The episode is worth retaining because it checks the tendency to write famous liners backward from their later reputations. Rex did not enter service already proven; reliability and prestige had to be earned after a visibly troubled beginning.

Blue Riband

On 16 August 1933 Rex reached New York after a record westbound passage from Gibraltar at an average 28.92 knots. The performance gave Italy the Blue Riband and displaced the German express liners from the top of the westbound record table until Normandie surpassed the mark in 1935.

The record was commercially useful, but it also carried political and cultural significance. In the interwar period, express liners were routinely presented as national technological showcases; Rex fit that pattern directly.

War and Destruction

After Italy entered the Second World War in June 1940, Rex ceased passenger service and was eventually moved from Trieste toward the Capodistria area. On 8 September 1944 Allied aircraft attacked the ship, leaving her burning and capsized in shallow water. The wreck was subsequently dismantled in place.

Descriptions of the final attack vary in minor operational detail, so this guide keeps the conclusion restrained: the ship was destroyed by Allied air attack in September 1944 and did not return to service.
