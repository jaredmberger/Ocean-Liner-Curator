RMS Britannic (1874)

White Star Line · entered service 1874 · Ship Guide

Overview

RMS Britannic was a larger development of White Star's first Oceanic-class generation and the elder sister of Germanic. Built by Harland & Wolff, launched on 3 February 1874 and entering Liverpool–New York service on 25 June, she combined large steerage capacity with better saloon accommodation and competitive performance.

Her 1876 record passages show that White Star's early “comfort” reputation existed alongside real participation in the Atlantic speed contest. Later, the ship became HMT 62 for Boer War troop transport before retirement.

Migration and Capacity

Britannic's large steerage accommodation is central to her meaning. Passenger lists and agent material document an Atlantic transport system built as much around migration as prestige travel.

1876 Record Passages

Contemporary and compiled histories credit Britannic with both eastbound and westbound record passages in 1876. Curatorial wording should preserve direction and date rather than treating “Blue Riband holder” as a permanent title.

HMT 62

Requisitioned during the South African War, Britannic moved into a military transport role very different from ordinary White Star service. Troopship photographs and documents should therefore be treated as a separate phase.

Interpretive Notes

This is the 1874 White Star liner, not the later 1914 hospital ship or the 1930 motor liner. Build year is essential whenever the name Britannic appears without further context.
