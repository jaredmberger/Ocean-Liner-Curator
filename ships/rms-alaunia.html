RMS Alaunia (1913)

Cunard Line · entered service 1913 · Ship Guide

Overview

RMS Alaunia was a medium-sized Cunard passenger-cargo liner built for the Boston and Canadian-oriented side of the company's North Atlantic business. Scotts at Greenock launched her on 9 June 1913; her commercial career lasted less than three years before war reshaped her employment.

A Practical Atlantic Liner

Alaunia belongs to Cunard's migration and intermediate-service infrastructure rather than its prestige flagships. Her large third-class capacity is more historically revealing than any claim to luxury or speed.

Wartime Shift

Government employment moved the ship into troop and military transport work. Route references from 1914–16 should therefore be treated separately from the short prewar passenger phase.

19 October 1916

uboat.net's monthly loss record identifies the mine as one laid by UC-16 under Egon von Werner. That specific attribution is stronger than generic wording that simply calls the ship a wartime mine loss.

Interpretive Notes

Because the ship's service life was short, undated ephemera deserve particular caution. A Boston-service brochure, troopship reference and loss account belong to sharply different contexts.
