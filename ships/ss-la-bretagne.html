SS La Bretagne

Compagnie Générale Transatlantique · 1886 · Ship Guide

Overview

SS La Bretagne was a French Line ocean liner built at Saint-Nazaire for Compagnie Générale Transatlantique’s Le Havre–New York service. Entering service in 1886, she belonged to the important late nineteenth-century group of CGT liners that helped modernize the company’s North Atlantic presence.

This page treats La Bretagne as a transitional French liner: steel-hulled, steam-driven, still visually tied to earlier sail-era forms, and later modified as marine engineering and passenger expectations changed.

Design & Construction Context

La Bretagne was part of a French Line fleet-renewal program that included ships such as La Champagne, La Bourgogne, and La Gascogne. These vessels helped CGT answer competitive pressure on the North Atlantic while meeting French mail-service requirements.

Her original appearance—with two funnels and four masts—shows the overlap between older visual traditions and modern steamship technology. The 1895 modernization, including reduced mast arrangement and improved machinery, placed her more firmly within the late nineteenth-century steam-liner profile.

Service History (Summary)

La Bretagne entered Le Havre–New York service in August 1886 and spent decades as part of the French Line’s Atlantic passenger network. She carried a mix of cabin passengers, emigrants, mail, and cargo during a period when France’s transatlantic service was becoming more regular and more competitive.

In 1912, she was sold to Compagnie de Navigation Sud-Atlantique for service between France and South America. She continued under her original name for part of that career before later operating as SS Alesia.

In 1923, the aging liner was sold for scrapping. While being towed toward the breakers, her towline parted and she grounded near Texel, becoming a total loss before demolition could proceed in the ordinary way.

Historical Significance

La Bretagne matters because she belongs to the generation that made French Line’s North Atlantic service more modern, consistent, and internationally visible before the company’s later prestige liners.

She also illustrates the extended working lives of nineteenth-century liners. A ship built for Le Havre–New York service could later be sold into South Atlantic work, renamed, and remain commercially useful long after newer liners had displaced her from the main prestige route.

Interpretive Notes

For collecting and research, La Bretagne material should be matched to phase: original CGT transatlantic service, post-1895 refit appearance, Compagnie de Navigation Sud-Atlantique service, or the later Alesia identity.

Photographs are especially date-sensitive because her mast arrangement changed. A four-masted image likely represents her earlier configuration, while a two-masted image points to the post-1895 appearance. That distinction can help avoid misdating postcards, prints, or album captions.
