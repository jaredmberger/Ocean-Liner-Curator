SS Conte Grande

Lloyd Sabaudo · entered service 1928 · Ship Guide

Overview

SS Conte Grande completed Lloyd Sabaudo's celebrated “Four Counts” sequence. Built by Stabilimento Tecnico Triestino at Trieste and launched on 29 June 1927, she entered Genoa–Naples–New York service on 13 April 1928. Her arrival marked the mature form of the company's 1920s prestige program immediately before Italian shipping was consolidated into Italia Flotte Riunite.

Her wartime career transformed the liner into USS Monticello (AP-61), a U.S. troop transport whose operations stretched from Operation Torch to the China–Burma–India route and Pacific bases. Returned to Italy after the war, she was rebuilt and resumed South American passenger service.

The Fourth Count

Italian Liners Historical Society treats Conte Grande as the fourth and culminating ship of Lloyd Sabaudo's “Conti” publicity family. Compared with the earlier Beardmore-built ships, she also illustrates the shift toward domestic Italian construction.

The former page incorrectly located Stabilimento Tecnico Triestino at Monfalcone. The builder was the Trieste concern, and preserving that distinction matters for the site's builder database as well as for historical accuracy.

From North Atlantic to South America

The ship began on Genoa–Naples–New York service, then moved into South American work after the 1932 consolidation that created Italia Flotte Riunite. Passenger lists, schedules and company literature therefore span both Lloyd Sabaudo and Italian Line identities and multiple route systems.

USS Monticello

Caught in Brazil by the widening war, the liner was interned and then passed into U.S. hands. Naval History records purchase on 16 April 1942 and commissioning as USS Monticello the same day. After conversion at Philadelphia she carried troops to Operation Torch, then to India via the Panama Canal, Australia and Ceylon, and later across both the Pacific and Atlantic.

The U.S. military phase should be cataloged independently from Italian passenger service. The name Monticello, Navy fittings and troop records represent a different institutional identity even though the hull remained the same.

Postwar Reconstruction

Returned to Italy in 1947, Conte Grande underwent extensive reconditioning and resumed South American service in 1949. GG Archives preserves both prewar and postwar passenger material, providing unusually good evidence for comparing the ship's two commercial eras.

She remained active until 1960, briefly passed into Lloyd Triestino service, and was scrapped in 1961.

Interpretive Notes

Conte Grande is particularly useful for studying corporate consolidation and wartime redistribution: Lloyd Sabaudo flagship, Italian Line South America liner, U.S. transport, and rebuilt postwar liner are all legitimate identities but should never be flattened into one undated description.
