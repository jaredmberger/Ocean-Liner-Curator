RMS Servia

Cunard Line · entered service 1881 · Ship Guide

Overview

RMS Servia was one of Cunard's most important transitional liners. Built by J. & G. Thomson at Clydebank and launched on 1 March 1881, she entered Liverpool–New York service that November at a moment when ocean-liner construction was moving decisively from iron toward steel.

Her significance is technological as much as commercial: Servia is widely identified as the first large ocean liner built of steel and the first Cunard ship fitted with electric lighting. Yet she still retained a barque rig and the visual language of earlier steamships, making her a particularly clear bridge between two generations.

A Threshold Ship

Servia matters because old and new coexist visibly in one vessel. Steel construction and electric lighting point forward, while a single screw and substantial sailing rig retain the habits of the preceding generation.

Passenger and Mail Service

Her long Cunard career centered on regular North Atlantic mail and passenger work. By the 1890s, newer express liners displaced her from the front rank, but that later secondary status should not obscure the scale of her original technological importance.

Interpretive Notes

The most useful curatorial distinction is chronological: early material documents a leading Cunard technological showcase; later material documents an aging liner displaced by newer express tonnage. Specifications and accommodation should therefore be tied to date where possible.
