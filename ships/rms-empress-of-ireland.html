RMS Empress of Ireland

Canadian Pacific · entered service 1906 · Ship Guide

Overview

RMS Empress of Ireland was one of Canadian Pacific's principal prewar Atlantic liners, built by Fairfield Shipbuilding & Engineering at Govan for the Liverpool–Quebec route. Launched on 27 January 1906, she entered service that June and completed 95 successful return voyages before her loss.

In the early hours of 29 May 1914, while outbound from Quebec for Liverpool, the ship collided in fog with the Norwegian collier Storstad near Rimouski. She sank in about fourteen minutes. Parks Canada records 1,012 deaths among 1,477 people aboard.

Migration and Canadian Service

Parks Canada emphasizes that tens of thousands of immigrants reached Canada aboard Empress of Ireland between 1906 and 1914. Her second- and third-class accommodation is therefore as important to her history as the better-known first-class public rooms.

29 May 1914

The collision with Storstad opened the starboard side in conditions that gave passengers and crew very little time to escape. The scale of the loss makes the wreck the worst peacetime maritime disaster in Canadian history.

The Wreck as Evidence

Parks Canada describes the wreck as the country's most structurally complete early-20th-century ocean liner and the sole surviving wreck of a pre-First World War Canadian Pacific liner. That status gives the site unusual archaeological importance in addition to its memorial role.

Interpretive Notes

The disaster should not erase eight years of routine migration and transport history. Curatorial treatment should preserve both the ordinary service life and the human catastrophe of the final voyage.
