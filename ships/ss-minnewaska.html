SS Minnewaska (1908)

Atlantic Transport Line · entered service 1909 · Ship Guide

Overview

SS Minnewaska was a later Atlantic Transport Line passenger-cargo liner built by Harland & Wolff at Belfast. Launched on 12 November 1908 and entering London–New York service in May 1909, she belonged to the line's practical mixed-service fleet rather than the North Atlantic express race.

Requisitioned during the First World War, she became a troop transport. On 29 November 1916 she struck a mine laid by UC-23 near Suda Bay, Crete. The damaged ship was successfully beached and her troops saved, but the hull was a total loss.

Atlantic Transport Line Model

Minnewaska represents the line's middle ground between cargo vessel and passenger liner: substantial freight capacity, respectable passenger accommodation and dependable rather than record-setting speed.

Troopship Service

During the war the ship carried troops and artillery in Mediterranean service. Her military phase should be separated from ordinary London–New York passenger material because the vessel's purpose, fittings and documentation changed substantially.

29 November 1916

First World War submarine-loss records attribute the mine to UC-23. After the explosion, the ship developed a severe list, but the master managed to run her ashore near the entrance to Suda Bay. The beaching prevented a potentially much greater troopship disaster.

Interpretive Notes

This is the 1908-built Minnewaska. Atlantic Transport reused the name for a 1923 liner, so the construction date is essential when cataloging photographs or ephemera.
