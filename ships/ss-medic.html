SS Medic

White Star Line · entered service 1899 · Ship Guide

Overview

SS Medic was one of White Star Line's Jubilee-class cargo-passenger ships built for the long Liverpool–Cape Town–Australia service. Harland & Wolff launched her on 15 December 1898, and she entered service in 1899.

The ship belonged to a very different White Star world from the North Atlantic express liners. The Australia service combined passengers with large refrigerated holds, wool and general cargo, and White Star's own 1907 brochure described the route as carrying one passenger class rather than the familiar Atlantic first/second/third-class hierarchy.

The Australia Service

White Star's 1907 fleet literature grouped Medic with Afric, Persic, Runic and Suevic as the five 12,000-ton ships of the Australian service. They sailed from Liverpool via Cape Town and returned through South African ports.

Cargo Was Central

Norway-Heritage's technical record notes refrigerating machinery as part of Medic's original equipment. Refrigerated meat and other produce were not secondary details: cargo capacity was fundamental to the economics of the route.

War and Hektoria

During the First World War Medic worked under wartime requisition arrangements while retaining the utility that made the class valuable. Sold in 1928, she was converted into the whale-factory ship Hektoria, a complete change of commercial purpose.

Interpretive Notes

White Star Australia-service material should not be labeled through North Atlantic assumptions. Route, cargo function and the one-class passenger model are essential context.
