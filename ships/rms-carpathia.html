RMS Carpathia

Cunard Line · entered service 1903 · Ship Guide

Overview

RMS Carpathia was a practical Cunard intermediate liner built for steady Atlantic service rather than record speed. Swan Hunter launched her on 6 August 1902, and she entered Liverpool–Queenstown–Boston service in May 1903.

Her place in public memory is dominated by 15 April 1912, when she reached the site of Titanic's sinking and embarked the survivors. That event deserves its prominence, but it should not erase a decade of migration-oriented service or the wartime career that followed.

Working Liner Before Rescue Ship

Carpathia's regular business was mass passenger transport. Her lower speed and large emigrant accommodation placed her outside Cunard's express prestige tier but squarely inside the social history of Atlantic migration.

15 April 1912

The rescue generated an unusually rich documentary trail: wireless messages, inquiry testimony, passenger accounts and contemporary reporting. Precise survivor totals vary depending on counting conventions, so this guide avoids treating one rounded number as more exact than the underlying records allow.

1918 Loss

uboat.net records Carpathia sunk by U-55 under Wilhelm Werner on 17 July 1918 while in ballast from Liverpool toward Boston. Five people were killed.

Interpretive Notes

A Cunard artifact from Carpathia is not automatically a Titanic artifact. Voyage date, printed route and provenance determine whether an object belongs to the rescue episode or to the ship's much broader commercial history.
