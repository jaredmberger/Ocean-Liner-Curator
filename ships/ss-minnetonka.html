SS Minnetonka (1902)

Atlantic Transport Line · entered service 1902 · Ship Guide

Overview

SS Minnetonka was one of Atlantic Transport Line's large early-twentieth-century London–New York passenger-cargo ships. Built by Harland & Wolff at Belfast and entering service in July 1902, she combined substantial cargo capacity with a deliberately limited first-class passenger business.

That operating model makes her an important counterpoint to the era's famous express liners. Atlantic Transport could market comfort and exclusivity without carrying steerage passengers, while still relying heavily on freight revenue.

First Class Without the Express Race

GG Archives' surviving passenger lists repeatedly identify Minnetonka as first-class-only passenger service on the London–New York route. That exclusivity should not be confused with a pure luxury-liner business: large cargo capacity remained fundamental to the ship's economics.

IMM Context

Atlantic Transport Line became part of International Mercantile Marine, placing Minnetonka inside the same corporate system as White Star and Red Star ships while retaining a distinct route and commercial identity.

1918 Loss

uboat.net's First World War loss tables identify U-64 under Robert Moraht as the submarine that sank Minnetonka on 30 January 1918. Some secondary summaries repeat U-66; this guide follows the more specific submarine-loss record.

Interpretive Notes

This is the 1902 Minnetonka, not the later 1924 Atlantic Transport liner of the same name. Date should accompany the name in ambiguous catalog records.
