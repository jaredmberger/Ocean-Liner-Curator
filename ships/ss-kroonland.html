SS Kroonland

Red Star Line · entered service 1902 · Ship Guide

Overview

SS Kroonland was an American-built Red Star liner completed by William Cramp & Sons at Philadelphia in 1902. At launch she was widely described as the largest steamship yet built in the United States, and she entered New York–Antwerp service that June.

Her career is unusually rich in communications, rescue and wartime history: early wireless episodes, participation in the 1913 rescue of survivors from the burning Volturno, a 1915 Panama Canal transit, and later service as both U.S. Army and Navy transport.

American Construction, Belgian Route

Kroonland demonstrates the international nature of Red Star Line under IMM: an American-built, American-flagged ship serving a route defined by Antwerp and New York. GG Archives preserves extensive passenger and schedule material from that world.

Wireless and Rescue

Contemporary reporting associated Kroonland with important early wireless episodes. More concretely documented is her role in the October 1913 Volturno disaster, when she took aboard 89 survivors despite rough conditions.

Panama Canal and War

Naval History and Heritage Command records Kroonland passing through the Panama Canal in February 1915 on her way to San Francisco. By 1917 she had an armed naval guard, and in 1918 she entered formal U.S. Navy transport service.

NHHC also records the striking 20 May 1917 encounter in which a torpedo hit the ship without exploding and the attacking submarine briefly scraped along her side before diving away.

Interpretive Notes

Kroonland's Red Star, American Line, Panama Pacific, Army and Navy phases should remain distinct. Corporate continuity under IMM does not erase differences in route, flag, mission or artifact context.
