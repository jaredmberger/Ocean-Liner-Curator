SS Columbus (1924)

Norddeutscher Lloyd · entered service 1924 · Ship Guide

Overview

SS Columbus was Norddeutscher Lloyd's largest prestige liner of the immediate postwar rebuilding era. Originally conceived before the First World War as Hindenburg, the ship was completed at Schichau's Danzig yard in 1924 after a long interruption and entered Bremen–New York service under the name Columbus.

She was both a liner and a major cruise ship, and a 1929 modernization substantially changed her machinery and appearance. Her career ended dramatically on 19 December 1939, when the crew scuttled her off the U.S. East Coast rather than allow British capture.

The Columbus Name Problem

This is the 1924 NDL Columbus. The earlier ship originally intended to carry the same name was ceded after World War I and became White Star's Homeric. The surviving German hull, originally intended as Hindenburg, inherited the Columbus name.

1929 Modernization

The 1929 refit was substantial enough to create a useful photographic breakpoint. New geared turbines increased speed, while shorter funnels and altered upperworks gave the ship a more contemporary appearance.

Cruising

Columbus became an important cruise ship as well as a transatlantic liner. Cruise ephemera should therefore be cataloged separately from scheduled North Atlantic material, even when both bear the same NDL branding.

1939 Scuttling

After the outbreak of war, Columbus sought to return to Germany. On 19 December 1939, faced with British interception off the American coast, the crew scuttled the ship. The neutral U.S. cruiser USS Tuscaloosa took aboard the German crew and brought them to the United States.

Interpretive Notes

Pre-1929 and post-1929 photographs can look significantly different. Name confusion with the earlier Columbus/Homeric makes date, machinery and silhouette especially important for attribution.
