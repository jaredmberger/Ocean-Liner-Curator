RMS Franconia (1910)

Cunard Line · entered service 1911 · Ship Guide

Overview

RMS Franconia was a large Cunard intermediate liner built by Swan Hunter for the Boston trade. Launched on 23 July 1910 and completed in January 1911, she entered service in February and became closely associated with Liverpool–Boston traffic, while also working New York and Mediterranean schedules.

The Boston Liner

Franconia was built specifically to strengthen Cunard's Boston service. Contemporary reporting on her first arrival emphasized both her scale and the unusually generous number of baths and showers aboard, evidence of how comfort improvements were being marketed below the express-liner tier.

Seasonal Versatility

The ship's route history includes Boston, New York and Mediterranean work. Passenger lists and brochures therefore need to be dated carefully; a generic “North Atlantic” label hides meaningful seasonal differences.

War and Loss

After government service began, Franconia was used as both a hospital ship and troop transport. On 4 October 1916 UB-47 torpedoed her while she was bound toward Salonika. Twelve of 314 crew died; survivors were picked up by Dover Castle.

Interpretive Notes

This is the 1910-launched Franconia, not Cunard's later ships of the same name. Construction date should always accompany the name where ambiguity is possible.
