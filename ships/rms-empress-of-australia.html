RMS Empress of Australia

Canadian Pacific · in CP service from 1922 · Ship Guide

Overview

RMS Empress of Australia began life as the German liner Tirpitz, launched by AG Vulcan at Stettin on 20 December 1913. War interrupted the intended Hamburg-America career, and the ship was ultimately ceded to Britain after Germany's defeat.

Canadian Pacific acquired the vessel in 1922, renamed her Empress of Australia, and rebuilt her for a new career that included both trans-Pacific and trans-Atlantic service. The ship later served as a troop transport in the Second World War and continued trooping into the Korean War era before disposal in 1952.

Tirpitz to Empress

The postwar transfer created a genuine new identity. German prewar material, British transitional references, and Canadian Pacific ephemera belong to different phases even though they concern the same hull.

Pacific and Atlantic Service

GG Archives identifies both trans-Pacific and trans-Atlantic employment. A 1928 Canadian Pacific passenger list also demonstrates the company's integrated rail-and-sea travel system, with shipboard railway ticketing used to connect ocean passengers directly into inland Canadian journeys.

Troopship Career

During the Second World War Empress of Australia served as a troopship, and GG Archives records continued troop transport through 1952. By then the aging liner had accumulated nearly four decades of physical life across German, British and Canadian contexts.

Interpretive Notes

This is the 1913-launched former Tirpitz, not the later ship that briefly bore the Empress of Australia name in the 1950s. Original name and launch year are the safest disambiguators.
