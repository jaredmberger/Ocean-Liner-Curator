RMS Strathmore

P&O · entered service 1935 · Ship Guide

Overview

RMS Strathmore was the third of P&O's five “Strath” liners of the 1930s and, when completed, the company's largest and fastest passenger ship. Vickers-Armstrong built her at Barrow-in-Furness; she was launched on 4 April 1935 by the Duchess of York and delivered on 15 September.

Her career covered prewar India and Australia service, extensive troopship duty, a postwar return to P&O, one-class tourist service, and a final life in Greek ownership as a pilgrim and hotel ship.

The Strath Generation

Strathmore followed Strathnaver and Strathaird and differed visually from those earlier three-funnel ships by having a single functional funnel. The white hull and buff funnel marked P&O's distinctive 1930s passenger-liner identity.

India and Australia

Her 1935 maiden London–Bombay sailing broke the Marseilles–Bombay passage record at roughly 20 knots. In 1936 she carried the incoming Viceroy of India, Lord Linlithgow, to Bombay and returned with Lord Willingdon.

Wartime Trooping

Requisitioned on 31 March 1940, Strathmore spent the war as a troop transport. P&O Heritage records her departure in March 1941 as part of the largest convoy then assembled, carrying 80,000 troops toward Suez. Postwar, she also repatriated New Zealand troops and released Australian prisoners of war.

Second Civilian Career

After reconditioning she returned to commercial service in October 1949. In 1961 she was converted to one-class accommodation for 1,200 tourist passengers. Sold in 1963, she became Marianna Latsi and later Henrietta Latsi.

Interpretive Notes

Prewar P&O, troopship, postwar tourist-class, and Greek hotel/pilgrim phases should be treated as separate provenance contexts even though the hull remained continuous.
