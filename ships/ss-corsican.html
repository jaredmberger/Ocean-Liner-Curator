SS Corsican

Allan Line · entered service 1907 · Ship Guide

Overview

SS Corsican was an Allan Line passenger-cargo liner built by Barclay, Curle & Co. at Glasgow and launched on 29 April 1907. She entered Liverpool–Saint John service later that year and soon became closely tied to Canadian Pacific through repeated charter arrangements.

When Canadian Pacific absorbed the Allan Line operation, Corsican effectively crossed from one historic Canadian route system into another. Renamed Marvale in 1922, she was wrecked off Newfoundland the following year.

Allan Line to Canadian Pacific

Norway-Heritage documents repeated Canadian Pacific charters from 1908 onward, making the later ownership transition less abrupt than a simple sale date suggests. Corsican is therefore a useful bridge between Allan Line and Canadian Pacific passenger history.

Marvale

The 1922 rename to Marvale creates a hard documentary boundary. Material under that name belongs to the final Canadian Pacific phase and should not be retroactively labeled Corsican.

Interpretive Notes

Allan Line, Canadian Pacific, and Marvale material may all refer to the same hull, but company imprint and date remain essential provenance evidence.
