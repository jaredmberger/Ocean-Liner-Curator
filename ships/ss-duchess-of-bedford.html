SS Duchess of Bedford

Canadian Pacific Steamships · entered service 1928 · Ship Guide

Overview

SS Duchess of Bedford was the first completed member of Canadian Pacific's four-ship Duchess class. Built by John Brown & Co. at Clydebank as yard no. 518, she entered Liverpool–Quebec–Montreal service on 1 June 1928.

Unlike two of her sisters, she survived the Second World War. After extensive troopship work she was rebuilt for civilian service and renamed Empress of France, returning to the Canadian route in 1948. The renaming creates an important provenance boundary: prewar Duchess material and postwar Empress material belong to one hull but different public identities.

One of Four Near-Sisters

The Duchess class was designed around Canadian Pacific's seasonal Britain–Canada service. Their similarities were commercially useful but create modern attribution problems: a generic two-funnel photograph can easily migrate from one sister to another in online captions.

GG Archives documents Duchess of Bedford passenger lists in 1931 and 1932 and preserves sailing schedules that place the class within the wider Canadian Pacific fleet rather than as isolated prestige ships.

War and Reconstruction

Requisitioned during the Second World War, Duchess of Bedford spent years in troop transport. That military employment heavily altered the ship's onboard use even where the external profile remained recognizable.

After the war Canadian Pacific chose to rebuild the ship for renewed transatlantic service. The postwar transformation included accommodation changes and a new public identity. GG Archives records the renamed Empress of France returning to Liverpool–Quebec–Montreal service in September 1948.

Empress of France

The later name can create confusion with the earlier Canadian Pacific Empress of France, formerly Alsatian. For cataloging, “Empress of France (ex-Duchess of Bedford)” is the safest label when the date is not obvious.

Interpretive Notes

The name change is not a footnote but a provenance break. A 1930 menu belongs to Duchess of Bedford; a 1953 passenger list belongs to Empress of France, even though both came from the same hull.
