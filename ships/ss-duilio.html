SS Duilio

Navigazione Generale Italiana · entered service 1923 · Ship Guide

Overview

SS Duilio was a major Navigazione Generale Italiana liner whose construction straddled the First World War. Laid down before the conflict and launched by Ansaldo at Sestri Ponente on 9 January 1916, she was not completed until 1923. That seven-year gap makes her both a prewar design and a distinctly postwar commercial ship.

Her route history is more varied than a simple “Genoa–Buenos Aires liner” label suggests. She began with New York service, shifted to South America, was rebuilt for the South African mail trade in the 1930s, passed into Lloyd Triestino control, and later undertook International Red Cross repatriation voyages before being sunk by Allied aircraft at Trieste in 1944.

A Ship Delayed by War

Duilio's launch date can be misleading if read without the completion date. The hull entered the water in 1916, but wartime constraints postponed completion until 1923. For chronology, “launched 1916” and “entered service 1923” are both essential.

Her close operating relationship with Giulio Cesare also matters. The two became an important pair in NGI service and later moved together through South American and South African assignments.

Changing Routes

Italian Liners Historical Society records Duilio's final New York crossing in 1928, after which she joined the Latin American trade. The 1932 creation of Italia Flotte Riunite changed the corporate context without immediately changing the ship's basic South American role.

In 1933–34 she underwent a substantial rebuild for the Genoa–Cape Town mail service. Passenger accommodation was reduced and reconfigured for the new market, illustrating how an existing prestige liner could be repurposed around a different contract rather than simply replaced.

Red Cross Repatriation and Loss

In 1942 Duilio was chartered for International Red Cross repatriation work, carrying Italian civilians from former East African colonies. These voyages were neither ordinary commercial sailings nor conventional troop transport and deserve their own interpretive category.

Laid up near Trieste after her final repatriation voyage, the ship was damaged and sunk by Allied aircraft on 10 July 1944. She was later refloated for demolition.

Interpretive Notes

The strongest way to catalog Duilio material is by phase: NGI/New York, NGI–Italian Line South America, South African mail service, and Red Cross repatriation. The same ship changed passengers, route, operator and purpose substantially across those phases.
