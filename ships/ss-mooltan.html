RMS Mooltan (1923)

P&O · entered service 1923 · Ship Guide

Overview

RMS Mooltan was the first of P&O's paired 20,000-ton postwar liners, preceding sister Maloja into service in 1923. Built by Harland & Wolff at Belfast as yard no. 587, she was launched on 15 February and completed that September.

Like Maloja, she was designed around the physical and commercial realities of the Suez route to India and Australia. P&O Heritage identifies her as the company's first ship over 20,000 gross tons.

Design and Machinery

Mooltan and Maloja deliberately used quadruple-expansion machinery for long-route reliability. Later machinery additions increased speed and efficiency without changing the basic commercial role.

Australia Service

Her regular work connected Britain with ports through the Mediterranean, Suez, Colombo and Australia. P&O material also preserves the ship's substantial first- and second-class public rooms, including a double-height tourist lounge after later accommodation changes.

War and Trooping

Requisitioned in 1939, Mooltan first served as an armed merchant cruiser and subsequently as a troop transport. She survived the war and returned to P&O.

Postwar Emigrant Role

After civilian reconditioning, Mooltan became strongly associated with postwar migrant traffic to Australia. That phase represents a different passenger market from her interwar two-class service and should be cataloged separately.
