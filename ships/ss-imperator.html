SS Imperator

Hamburg-America Line · entered service 1913 · Ship Guide

Overview

SS Imperator was the first of Hamburg-America Line's three giant prewar liners and, at completion, one of the largest ships in the world. Built by A.G. Vulcan at Hamburg, launched on 23 May 1912, and entering service on 18 June 1913, she inaugurated HAPAG's attempt to compete through unprecedented scale rather than outright record speed.

Her short German passenger career was followed by wartime lay-up, postwar Allied use, and eventual transfer to Cunard. Renamed Berengaria in 1921, she then began an entirely separate British career.

Scale Instead of Speed

The Imperator class represented a strategic shift for HAPAG. Rather than chase the absolute fastest crossing, the company emphasized enormous volume, elaborate public rooms and a highly visible sense of scale. GG Archives' period material records accommodation for thousands of passengers across multiple classes.

Stability and Early Corrections

Imperator's early service revealed excessive top-heaviness. Weight high in the ship was reduced and ballast added low in the hull. The famous large eagle figurehead at the bow also suffered damage and was eventually removed. These changes are important photographic dating markers.

War and Transfer

When war began in 1914, Imperator remained in Germany and ceased commercial service. After the conflict she was surrendered under the postwar settlement and used for repatriation work before passing to Cunard.

The later Berengaria identity belongs to a new operator, livery and passenger culture. Because Ocean Liner Curator already treats Berengaria separately, this page deliberately stops the main narrative at the transfer boundary.

Interpretive Notes

Prewar Imperator material should not be retroactively labeled Berengaria. The name change marks one of the clearest operator/provenance breaks in ocean-liner history.
