SS Belgenland

Red Star Line · passenger service from 1923 · Ship Guide

Overview

SS Belgenland was the largest and most ambitious liner associated with the Red Star Line. Launched by Harland & Wolff at Belfast on 31 December 1914, she was conceived before the First World War but completed in 1917 in a wartime cargo configuration as Belgic for White Star Line. Only after the war was the hull rebuilt into the passenger liner originally intended.

Returned to Red Star Line and renamed Belgenland, she entered passenger service in 1923 and became the company's flagship. Her career combined Antwerp–New York work with long round-the-world cruises that produced an unusually rich documentary trail of passenger lists, brochures, menus, route maps and port-specific material.

A Ship Whose Identity Forked

Belgenland's chronology cannot be reduced to a single launch-to-scrap narrative. The 1914 launch, 1917 completion as Belgic, and 1923 reconstruction as Belgenland describe materially different states of the same hull. Wartime photographs, White Star cargo records and later Red Star passenger ephemera therefore belong to distinct phases.

Red Star Flagship

GG Archives identifies Belgenland as the “Queen of the Red Star Line Fleet” and preserves substantial 1920s material showing her role on Antwerp–New York service. Contemporary brochures emphasized first- and second-class comfort while still accommodating a large third-class complement.

Round-the-World Cruising

Beginning in the mid-1920s, Belgenland became strongly associated with long world cruises from New York. These voyages are a major part of her historical identity because they illustrate the interwar shift from migration-heavy liner economics toward leisure travel and destination-based marketing.

Columbia

In 1935 the aging ship was transferred within the IMM group, placed under Panama Pacific Line management and renamed Columbia. She operated cruises from New York before being laid up and sent for demolition the following year.

Interpretive Notes

The safest attribution sequence is: projected Belgenland → wartime Belgic → passenger Belgenland → Columbia. Date and physical state matter as much as name.
