SS Manchuria

Pacific Mail Steamship Company · entered service 1904 · Ship Guide

Overview

SS Manchuria was a large American-built passenger-cargo liner constructed by New York Shipbuilding at Camden, New Jersey, for Pacific Mail's trans-Pacific system. Entering service in 1904, she linked San Francisco with Honolulu, Japan, China and the Philippines and demonstrates that ocean-liner history extends well beyond the North Atlantic.

Her long career included a 1906 grounding off Oahu, First World War service as USS Manchuria, interwar renaming as President Johnson, renewed troop transport during the Second World War, and a final postwar identity as Santa Cruz.

Pacific Mail Context

Manchuria was designed for mixed passenger, mail and cargo work across the Pacific. Her large steerage capacity is especially important because it places migration and labor mobility alongside first-class travel and diplomatic traffic.

1906 Oahu Grounding

In August 1906 the ship grounded off Oahu. The incident generated contemporary salvage, legal and newspaper documentation and should be treated as a discrete event rather than attached loosely to any undated postcard or photograph.

USS Manchuria

During the First World War she entered U.S. Navy service as USS Manchuria. Naval History preserves photographs of the ship returning American personnel from Europe in 1919, providing direct evidence for that transport phase.

President Johnson

Renamed President Johnson in 1928, the former Manchuria later operated in Dollar Line and American President Lines contexts. NHHC also documents her civilian-manned troopship use during the Second World War. These later identities should be cataloged separately from Pacific Mail material.

Interpretive Notes

The correct provenance chain is Pacific Mail Manchuria → USS Manchuria → President Johnson → Santa Cruz. Hull continuity does not erase operator, route or mission changes.
