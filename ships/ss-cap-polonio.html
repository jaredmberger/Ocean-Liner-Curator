SS Cap Polonio

Hamburg Süd · passenger service from 1922 · Ship Guide

Overview

SS Cap Polonio was Hamburg Süd's first liner over 20,000 gross tons and was intended for the Hamburg–Buenos Aires route. Launched by Blohm & Voss on 25 March 1914, she was still incomplete when the First World War interrupted construction.

Her early history is unusually fragmented: brief naval conversion as SMS Vineta, postwar operation under British control, unsuccessful Union-Castle and P&O employment, and finally repurchase by Hamburg Süd. Only in February 1922 did she begin the South Atlantic passenger service for which she had originally been designed.

A Delayed Civilian Career

Cap Polonio is a useful example of how war could separate launch date from true commercial entry. The ship existed physically from 1914, but her intended Hamburg Süd passenger career did not begin until eight years later.

Vineta and British Control

The Imperial German Navy briefly commissioned the ship as the auxiliary cruiser SMS Vineta. After the war she passed to British control and was tried first under Union-Castle and then P&O management, but recurring machinery problems made these experiments unsatisfactory.

Return to Hamburg Süd

Hamburg Süd repurchased the vessel in 1921. From February 1922 she finally operated between Germany and the River Plate and became one of the company's most prestigious South Atlantic passenger ships until the Depression undermined the economics of such large tonnage.

Interpretive Notes

The chronology should be cataloged as launched 1914 → SMS Vineta → British-managed postwar phase → Hamburg Süd passenger service from 1922. Using only the launch year obscures most of the ship's actual commercial history.
