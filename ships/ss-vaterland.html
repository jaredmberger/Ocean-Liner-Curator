SS Vaterland

Hamburg-America Line · entered service 1914 · Ship Guide

Overview

SS Vaterland was the second and largest-completed member of Hamburg-America Line's Imperator class. Built by Blohm & Voss at Hamburg, she entered service in May 1914 and had only a brief commercial career before the First World War trapped her at Hoboken, New Jersey.

When the United States entered the war on 6 April 1917, the ship was seized, transferred to the U.S. Navy, and renamed USS Leviathan in September. Her subsequent American troopship and passenger-liner career is so extensive that it constitutes a separate identity rather than merely a later chapter of Vaterland.

The Second Imperator-Class Giant

Vaterland took the HAPAG scale strategy even further than Imperator. Her vast passenger spaces and machinery were designed for the same prestige model: size, comfort and capacity rather than an all-out speed record.

War at Hoboken

The outbreak of war in August 1914 found Vaterland in New York-area waters. Rather than risk capture at sea, the ship remained laid up at Hoboken for nearly three years. That static period is historically significant because the vessel was physically present in the United States while still German-owned.

Seizure and Leviathan

Naval History and Heritage Command records U.S. seizure on 6 April 1917, transfer to Navy custody that June, and formal renaming as Leviathan on 6 September. In wartime service she became one of the most important American troop transports, completing ten round trips to Europe before the Armistice.

Interpretive Notes

This page treats Vaterland and Leviathan as distinct provenance phases. The same hull served both identities, but flag, owner, mission, fittings and public meaning changed fundamentally after seizure.
