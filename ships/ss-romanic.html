SS Romanic

White Star Line · in White Star service from 1903 · Ship Guide

Overview

SS Romanic began as Dominion Line's New England, built by Harland & Wolff and launched on 7 April 1898. After the IMM reorganization she transferred to White Star Line in 1903 and was renamed Romanic.

Her White Star career belonged to the line's secondary passenger network rather than the New York express service. She worked Liverpool–Boston and then Boston–Mediterranean routes, linking immigrant traffic, southern Europe and New England.

White Star by Transfer

Romanic was not ordered as a White Star ship. That matters: her transfer from Dominion Line reflects IMM's ability to move existing tonnage to whatever route needed capacity.

Mediterranean Traffic

GG Archives preserves a 1908 passenger list for Romanic sailing Genoa–Boston via Naples, Almeria and the Azores, and broader schedules show her alongside Canopic, Cretic, Republic and other White Star ships on Mediterranean service.

Scandinavian

Sold to Allan Line in 1912 and renamed Scandinavian, the ship entered another documentary life. Later Canadian Pacific control extended that afterlife beyond the White Star era.

Interpretive Notes

New England, Romanic and Scandinavian should be treated as distinct operating identities. The printed company name and voyage route are as important as the hull itself.
