SS Drottningholm

Swedish American Line · in Swedish service from 1920 · Ship Guide

Overview

SS Drottningholm began life as Allan Line's Virginian, an early turbine-powered North Atlantic liner completed in 1905. Swedish American Line acquired the ship in 1920, renamed and refitted her, and placed her on the Gothenburg–New York route.

Her long Swedish career included migration, family travel and ordinary transatlantic service, but during the Second World War she acquired a distinct diplomatic role as a neutral exchange and repatriation ship operating under negotiated safe conduct.

Virginian to Drottningholm

The Swedish identity was a genuine second life, not merely a name change. Route, company, passenger market and visual presentation all changed after acquisition. Material from the Allan Line/Canadian phase and Swedish American Line phase should therefore remain separately cataloged.

Gothenburg–New York

Passenger-list evidence documents Drottningholm as part of Swedish American Line's direct national connection with the United States. The ship helped carry migrants, visitors and returning travelers through the interwar years.

World War II Exchange Voyages

Sweden's neutrality gave Drottningholm a special wartime function. She undertook negotiated repatriation and exchange voyages involving civilians, diplomats and internees. Those voyages belong to a diplomatic transport context distinct from ordinary commercial sailings.

Interpretive Notes

A wartime passenger on Drottningholm was not automatically a refugee, prisoner of war or exchanged diplomat. Each voyage should be identified by date, ports and chartering or governmental context before assigning a more specific category.
