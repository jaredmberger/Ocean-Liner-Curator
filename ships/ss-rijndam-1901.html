SS Rijndam (1901)

Holland America Line · entered service 1901 · Ship Guide

Overview

SS Rijndam was one of Holland America Line's large early-twentieth-century passenger and emigrant steamers. Built by Harland & Wolff at Belfast as yard no. 336, launched on 18 May 1901, and completed that October, she belonged to the same broad generation as Potsdam and Noordam: sturdy twin-screw ships optimized for dependable Rotterdam–New York service rather than record speed.

Her long career reached far beyond routine line voyages. She survived a major collision in 1915, mine damage in 1916, served in the U.S. Navy as USS Rijndam during 1918–19, returned to Holland America, and in 1926–27 undertook the company's first round-the-world voyage as a floating university.

Migration and the “Spotless Fleet”

Holland America describes the company as a major carrier of European emigrants to the New World from the 1880s through the 1920s. Rijndam's accommodation makes that role visible: roughly 1,800 third-class berths compared with only a few hundred cabin berths. Migration, cargo, mail and cabin traffic were all central to the ship's economics.

Contemporary English-language material often spells the name Ryndam. That is not a different ship; both forms occur in period publicity and records.

Accidents Before U.S. Service

In May 1915 Rijndam was seriously damaged in a fog collision south of Nantucket, and in January 1916 she struck a mine in the Thames Estuary. She survived both incidents and returned to service. These episodes belong to the hazards of wartime neutral shipping and should not be conflated with her later U.S. naval role.

USS Rijndam

Naval History and Heritage Command records that the United States seized the Dutch ship in March 1918 and commissioned her on 1 May. She made six convoy voyages to Europe before the Armistice and then seven round trips returning personnel from France. NHHC also records a near miss by torpedoes on 31 May 1918 during the same episode in which USS President Lincoln was sunk.

The Navy decommissioned Rijndam and returned her to her owner in October 1919. The wartime archive therefore represents a sharply distinct institutional phase within the same hull biography.

The Floating University

Holland America's own anniversary history identifies Rijndam's 1926 voyage as the company's first round-the-world voyage and as a floating-university experiment. GG Archives records more than 500 students aboard. The episode gave an aging emigrant liner an unusually modern educational role late in her career.

Interpretive Notes

Rijndam is important precisely because she represents the practical center of liner history: migration, cargo, war service and adaptive reuse rather than headline luxury alone.
