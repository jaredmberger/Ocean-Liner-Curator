SS Cap Trafalgar

Hamburg Süd · entered service 1914 · Ship Guide

Overview

SS Cap Trafalgar was Hamburg Süd's large new liner for the Germany–River Plate trade, built by AG Vulcan at Hamburg and launched on 31 July 1913. She entered civilian service in 1914, but the outbreak of the First World War almost immediately ended her intended passenger career.

Converted into the auxiliary cruiser SMS Cap Trafalgar, she fought the British armed merchant cruiser HMS Carmania off Trindade on 14 September 1914 and was sunk. The action is notable as one of the earliest and most famous battles between converted ocean liners.

South Atlantic Flagship

Cap Trafalgar belonged to Hamburg Süd's effort to raise the profile of its Europe–South America passenger service. Her very brief civilian career makes surviving prewar passenger material unusually important for distinguishing intended commercial identity from later wartime fame.

Conversion to Auxiliary Cruiser

After war began, the liner was armed for commerce-raiding service. The conversion altered both appearance and mission, so photographs and documents from the naval phase should be cataloged separately from Hamburg Süd passenger material.

Battle with Carmania

On 14 September 1914, off Trindade in the South Atlantic, Cap Trafalgar met HMS Carmania, itself a converted Cunard liner. The two fought at close range for more than an hour before Cap Trafalgar sank. Royal Museums Greenwich preserves visual material associated with the action.

Interpretive Notes

Because the ship's civilian career lasted only months, later retellings can make her seem primarily a warship. This guide keeps the sequence explicit: Hamburg Süd passenger liner first, auxiliary cruiser second.
