RMS Empress of Russia

Canadian Pacific · entered service 1913 · Ship Guide

Overview

RMS Empress of Russia was a Canadian Pacific trans-Pacific turbine liner built by Fairfield at Govan as yard no. 484. Launched on 28 August 1912 and completed in March 1913, she and sister Empress of Asia represented a new level of speed and prestige on the Vancouver–East Asia route.

Pacific Speed and Prestige

GG Archives credits Empress of Russia with a Yokohama-to-Race Rocks crossing of 8 days, 18 hours, 31 minutes at an average 19.86 knots. The exact record context is route-specific, but it demonstrates that Canadian Pacific's Pacific service had its own speed culture.

War Service

Requisitioned as an armed merchant cruiser, the ship served in Far Eastern and Indian Ocean operations. One notable episode was carrying survivors from the German cruiser Emden after the Battle of Cocos.

1923 Yokohama Earthquake

Empress of Russia was present at Yokohama during the Great Kanto earthquake and became involved in rescue and relief activity. That event should be treated as a distinct disaster-response phase rather than allowed to overshadow the ship's long scheduled Pacific career.

Interpretive Notes

Atlantic assumptions fit poorly here. The ship belonged to Canadian Pacific's rail-and-Pacific system linking western Canada with East Asia, and her passenger and route context should be read accordingly.
