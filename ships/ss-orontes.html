SS Orontes (1929)

Orient Line · entered service 1929 · Ship Guide

Overview

SS Orontes was the fifth and last of Orient Line's broadly similar 20,000-ton liners of the 1920s. Built by Vickers-Armstrong at Barrow-in-Furness, she was launched on 27 February 1929 and delivered that September.

She served the England–Australia route, cruised during the interwar years, became a troopship from 1940 to 1947, and then returned to commercial service after a major postwar refit.

The Last of the Series

P&O Heritage describes Orontes as the last of Orient Line's five generally similar new liners of the 1920s, following Orama, Oronsay, Otranto and Osterley. Her raked stem distinguished her visually from the earlier ships.

Cruising and Australia Service

Like other interwar liners, Orontes combined scheduled Australia voyages with cruises. Her accommodation changed during the 1930s as tourist-class travel became more important.

War and Reconditioning

From 1940 through 1947 she served as a troopship. After the war she was extensively reconditioned and returned to the Australia route in 1948, making postwar interior and passenger-capacity figures distinct from the prewar ship.

Interpretive Notes

This is the 1929 Orontes, not the earlier 1902 Orient liner. Date should always accompany the name where ambiguity is possible.
