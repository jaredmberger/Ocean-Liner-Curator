SS Duchess of York

Canadian Pacific Steamships · entered service 1929 · Ship Guide

Overview

SS Duchess of York was the last of Canadian Pacific's four Duchess-class liners to enter service. Built by John Brown & Co. at Clydebank as yard no. 524, she was launched on 28 September 1928 and made her first voyage in March 1929.

Her peacetime work included both regular Canada service and cruises from New York. During the Second World War she became a troop transport and was fatally attacked by German aircraft off the Spanish coast on 11 July 1943.

Canada Service and Cruising

Like her sisters, Duchess of York was built around the seasonal St. Lawrence trade rather than the New York express race. She also demonstrates how quickly cruising became part of Canadian Pacific's interwar business.

GG Archives preserves a first-class passenger list for a March 1931 New York–Hamilton, Bermuda cruise, giving direct evidence of that leisure role.

Wartime Trooping

Requisition transformed the ship's purpose from scheduled passenger service to military transport. Her wartime material should therefore be treated as its own provenance phase, especially where peacetime Canadian Pacific branding had disappeared.

1943 Air Attack

On 11 July 1943, while in the Atlantic off the Spanish coast, Duchess of York was attacked by high-flying German Focke-Wulf aircraft. The ship was badly damaged and ultimately lost. GG Archives' fleet summary records eleven deaths.

The mechanism matters: unlike sister Duchess of Atholl, lost to a submarine, Duchess of York was destroyed by air attack. Grouping all wartime liner losses under generic “torpedoed” language would erase a meaningful distinction.

Interpretive Notes

The four Duchess ships were near-sisters, but their wartime endings were very different. Attribution should preserve individual ship identity rather than treating the class as interchangeable.
