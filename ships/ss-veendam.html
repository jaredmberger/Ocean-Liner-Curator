SS Veendam

Holland America Line · entered service 1923 · Ship Guide

Overview

SS Veendam was one of Holland America Line's first-generation turbine liners. Built by Harland & Wolff at Govan as yard no. 650, launched on 18 November 1922, and entering service in April 1923, she formed a close pair with sister Volendam.

Her career crossed an unusually sharp wartime break. After prewar Rotterdam–New York service and early Caribbean cruising, she was trapped at Rotterdam in May 1940, later seized by German forces, used as an accommodation ship for U-boat personnel, repeatedly damaged by Allied air raids, salvaged after the war, and then returned to Holland America service.

Turbine-Era Holland America

Veendam and Volendam marked Holland America's move into geared turbine propulsion on substantial passenger ships. They were not high-speed express liners; their approximately 15-knot service profile was matched to dependable North Atlantic schedules, mixed-class passenger traffic and cargo.

Holland America's timeline credits Veendam with the line's first Caribbean cruise, an important sign that cruising was becoming an intentional part of the business rather than an occasional novelty.

1940 and German Seizure

When Germany invaded the Netherlands in May 1940, Veendam lay at Rotterdam with Statendam. Holland America records both ships as damaged during fighting around the port. Unlike Statendam, however, Veendam survived sufficiently intact to be requisitioned by German forces.

She was subsequently used as an accommodation ship, including service at Gotenhafen and Hamburg for personnel associated with the German naval establishment. Allied air raids repeatedly damaged the vessel, and by 1945 she was partly sunk in Hamburg harbor.

Salvage and Return

After the German surrender, the ship was raised, made watertight and towed back to the Netherlands. She returned to commercial service in 1947. That postwar phase is a genuine second life: wartime photographs and German accommodation-ship documentation should not be cataloged as ordinary Holland America passenger material.

Interpretive Notes

Veendam's paired design with Volendam creates obvious visual-attribution risk. Date, printed name and route are stronger evidence than silhouette alone.
