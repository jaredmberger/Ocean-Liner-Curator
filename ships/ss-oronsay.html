SS Oronsay (1925)

Orient Line · entered service 1925 · Ship Guide

Overview

SS Oronsay was the second of Orient Line's five large 20,000-ton passenger ships built during the 1920s. John Brown & Co. at Clydebank built her as yard no. 500; she was launched on 16 August 1924, delivered in January 1925, and sailed from Tilbury for Brisbane on 7 February.

Her peacetime career combined Australia line voyages and cruising. Requisitioned in 1940 as a troopship, she took part in wartime evacuation and transport work until her loss in the South Atlantic in October 1942.

Orient Line's 1920s Fleet

Oronsay belonged to the major Orient Line rebuilding program that also produced Orama, Otranto, Osterley and Orontes. Her employment combined UK–Australia mail service with cruising.

Troopship and Evacuation Work

Requisitioned on 6 April 1940, she soon undertook troop movements and evacuation work. P&O Heritage records damage at St Nazaire while she was evacuating troops and survivors from the Lancastria disaster.

1942 Loss

On 9 October 1942 the Italian submarine Archimede attacked Oronsay in the South Atlantic. uboat.net records multiple torpedo hits before the liner finally sank that evening. Of 466 aboard, six were killed and 460 survived.

Interpretive Notes

This is the 1925 Oronsay, not the later 1950 Orient liner of the same name. Date is essential for image and ephemera attribution.
