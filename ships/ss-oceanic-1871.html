SS Oceanic (1871)

White Star Line · entered service 1871 · Ship Guide

Overview

SS Oceanic was the ship that announced the new White Star Line's design philosophy to the North Atlantic. Built by Harland & Wolff at Belfast, launched on 27 August 1870, and entering Liverpool–New York service on 2 March 1871, she combined an iron hull, steam propulsion and auxiliary sail with passenger arrangements that emphasized comfort and proportion.

Her importance is less about a single record than about influence. White Star's early reputation for steadiness, accommodation and visual coherence begins here, before the line's later record ships and giant liners.

A New White Star Identity

Oceanic was not merely the first ship in a sequence; she established the commercial and aesthetic vocabulary White Star would repeatedly develop. Her accommodation and proportions were promoted as evidence that a liner could compete on passenger experience as well as speed.

Atlantic to Pacific

After only a few years on the North Atlantic, Oceanic transferred into Pacific service under charter to Occidental and Oriental. That later phase belongs to a different route and operator context and should be cataloged separately from White Star Atlantic material.

Interpretive Notes

The name Oceanic was reused by White Star in 1899. Date and route are essential: “Oceanic” alone is not sufficient attribution for photographs, printed ephemera or fittings.
