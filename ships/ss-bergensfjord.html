SS Bergensfjord

Norwegian America Line · entered service 1913 · Ship Guide

Overview

SS Bergensfjord was one of the foundational liners of Norwegian America Line, built to give Norway a direct passenger connection with New York. Cammell Laird launched her at Birkenhead on 8 April 1913, and she began her first voyage from Kristiania on 25 September.

Her career is especially valuable because it spans several distinct systems: Scandinavian migration, interwar liner service and cruising, Allied wartime troop transport, and postwar South American and Israeli service under new names.

A Direct Norwegian Route

Bergensfjord and Kristianiafjord gave Norwegian travelers a direct national route to New York rather than requiring the continental connections common in earlier migration. Norway-Heritage documents the ship's large third-class accommodation and long emigrant role.

Neutrality and Interwar Service

Norway remained neutral during the First World War, allowing Bergensfjord to continue serving the Atlantic. Surviving passenger lists document direct Norway–New York voyages during the conflict and throughout the interwar period.

Second World War and Later Names

During the Second World War the ship passed into Allied transport service. Afterward the hull moved through new commercial identities, including Argentina, Jerusalem and Aliya, before final demolition.

Interpretive Notes

This ship matters precisely because it was not a giant express liner. Its large third-class spaces, national route and long emigrant career reveal the practical infrastructure of Scandinavian migration and family travel.
