RMS Maloja (1923)

P&O · entered service 1923 · Ship Guide

Overview

RMS Maloja was one of P&O's major post-First World War Australia liners and a close sister to Mooltan. Built by Harland & Wolff at Belfast as yard no. 588, she was launched on 19 April 1923, delivered on 25 October, and made her first sailing to Bombay on 2 November.

P&O designed the pair as the largest ships that could comfortably pass through the Suez Canal. Their conservative quadruple-expansion machinery reflected a deliberate preference for reliability on long imperial routes rather than prestige speed.

Suez and the Australia Route

Maloja's dimensions were shaped by the Suez Canal and the long Britain–India–Australia route. P&O's own fact sheet records the London–Colombo–Melbourne–Sydney service beginning in January 1924.

War Service

Requisitioned in September 1939, Maloja became an armed merchant cruiser and joined Northern Patrol work. In November 1941 she returned to P&O control for conversion to troopship service and later participated in the North African and Italian campaigns.

Postwar Return

Returned to P&O in January 1947, she was reconditioned and resumed commercial service in June 1948. Her postwar work included emigrant transport and the repatriation of Dutch nationals from Indonesia.

Interpretive Notes

The wartime removal and later replacement of her second, dummy funnel provides a useful visual dating clue. Prewar, armed-merchant-cruiser, troopship and postwar images should not be treated as one unchanging appearance.
