SS Marquette

Atlantic Transport Line · entered service 1898 · Ship Guide

Overview

SS Marquette was a late-Victorian cargo-passenger steamer built by Alexander Stephen & Sons at Linthouse, Glasgow. Launched in 1897 as Boadicea, she entered service in January 1898 and soon passed into Atlantic Transport Line service under the name Marquette.

She represents the commercially important but less glamorous side of transatlantic shipping: cargo, livestock and a relatively small first-class passenger complement. Her later Red Star charter work and First World War transport service added further identities before her loss in the Aegean in October 1915.

A Practical Atlantic Liner

Marquette carried passengers, but she was not a prestige express liner. Her economic purpose depended on cargo and livestock as much as saloon accommodation, making her a useful reminder that the transatlantic system was supported by many mixed-service vessels.

Red Star Charter

Period passenger material places Marquette in Atlantic Transport service, while later records also connect her with Red Star Line charter operations. Operator and date should therefore be preserved together when cataloging surviving ephemera.

The Marquette Disaster

On 23 October 1915 a German torpedo struck the troop transport as she entered the Gulf of Salonika. New Zealand's Ministry for Culture and Heritage records that the ship sank within about ten minutes and that 167 people died, including 32 New Zealanders. Ten of the dead were members of the New Zealand Army Nursing Service.

The medical personnel were travelling aboard an unmarked troop transport rather than the available hospital ship, a decision that later drew official criticism in New Zealand. This context makes the loss a human and administrative history, not simply a ship casualty.

Interpretive Notes

The strongest provenance sequence is Boadicea → Atlantic Transport Marquette → Red Star charter period → wartime transport. The 1915 disaster should not be projected backward onto ordinary peacetime artifacts.
