SS Volendam

Holland America Line · entered service 1922 · Ship Guide

Overview

SS Volendam was the first-completed half of Holland America Line's closely matched Volendam/Veendam pair. Built by Harland & Wolff at Govan as yard no. 649, she was launched on 6 July 1922, completed in October, and began Rotterdam–New York service on 4 November.

Her wartime history is especially significant. In August 1940, while carrying hundreds of civilian evacuees—including more than 300 children—she was torpedoed in the Western Approaches. The ship survived, and nearly everyone aboard was rescued. After repair she continued in Allied transport service before returning to Holland America after the war.

Sister to Veendam

Volendam and Veendam shared machinery, dimensions and much of their accommodation plan. GG Archives preserves photographs of public rooms explicitly captioned for both ships, a reminder that interiors can be just as difficult to distinguish as exterior profiles when ship-specific captions are absent.

Interwar Service and Cruising

In the 1920s and 1930s Volendam worked the Rotterdam–Hoboken route and also made seasonal cruises. Her class arrangements were revised repeatedly as the migration market declined and tourist-class travel grew.

The 1940 Evacuation Voyage

In late August 1940 Volendam sailed from Liverpool in convoy OB 205 carrying civilian evacuees, including more than 300 children. A German U-boat attack in the Western Approaches put a torpedo into the forward part of the ship and flooded holds. The master ordered abandonment.

The children and other passengers were transferred to lifeboats and rescued by nearby ships. The episode is often remembered because the children survived, but the safest formulation preserves the whole record: the ship was badly damaged, one passenger-side fatality is recorded in detailed accounts, and the evacuation succeeded on a very large scale.

Wartime and Postwar Return

Volendam was salvaged, repaired and returned to Allied transport work. After the war she was refurbished again for civilian service, including large-scale tourist-class accommodation, before final withdrawal.

Interpretive Notes

The 1940 story is strong enough that it can overwhelm the rest of the ship's career. In cataloging, civilian evacuation material, military transport records and prewar Holland America ephemera should remain distinct documentary categories.
