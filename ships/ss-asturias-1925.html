RMS Asturias (1925)

Royal Mail Steam Packet Company / Royal Mail Lines · entered service 1926 · Ship Guide

Overview

RMS Asturias was the first of Royal Mail's large postwar South America pair, followed by sister Alcantara. Harland & Wolff launched her at Belfast on 7 July 1925 and completed her in February 1926.

Originally a motor liner, she was extensively rebuilt in 1934 with steam turbines and a substantially altered external profile. During the Second World War she served as HMS Asturias, an armed merchant cruiser, until an Italian submarine torpedo crippled her in 1943. She later returned as an emigrant and troop transport.

Motor Liner to Turbine Liner

The 1934 machinery conversion is one of the most important facts for identification. The original diesel-powered ship had two low funnels; the rebuilt turbine ship presented a markedly different silhouette and greater speed.

South America Service

Asturias and Alcantara were built for the Southampton–South America route and represented Royal Mail's return to large prestige passenger tonnage after the First World War.

HMS Asturias

Requisitioned in 1939, she became an armed merchant cruiser. In 1943 the Italian submarine Cagni torpedoed the ship in the South Atlantic. Although gravely damaged, she survived and was later recovered for further use.

Postwar Emigrant and Troopship Service

After the war Asturias was converted for large-scale emigrant carriage, especially to Australia, and later served as a troopship. These austere postwar configurations bear little resemblance to the interwar Royal Mail passenger product.

A Final Film Footnote

While being broken up at Faslane, one side of the ship was used for filming scenes for the 1958 feature A Night to Remember, painted to represent Titanic. This should be treated as a final reuse of the physical hull, not as part of her operating career.

Interpretive Notes

This 1925-built Asturias must be kept separate from the 1908 Royal Mail liner of the same name. Date and machinery configuration are strong disambiguators.
