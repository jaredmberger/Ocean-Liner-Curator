SS Conte Verde

Lloyd Sabaudo · entered service 1923 · Ship Guide

Overview

SS Conte Verde was the second of Lloyd Sabaudo's celebrated early-1920s “Count” liners. Built by William Beardmore & Co. at Dalmuir and entering service in 1923, she combined large migrant capacity with first- and second-class accommodation on routes linking Italy with South America and, later, the Far East.

Her career became unusually international. She carried participants and officials to the first FIFA World Cup in Uruguay in 1930, later passed into Lloyd Triestino's East Asian network, served as a neutral diplomatic-exchange ship during the Second World War, and was scuttled at Shanghai after the 1943 Italian armistice before a complicated Japanese-controlled afterlife.

The “Count” Fleet

Conte Verde followed Conte Rosso and helped establish the Lloyd Sabaudo “Conti” identity that later expanded through Conte Biancamano and Conte Grande. Italian Liners Historical Society preserves the company's deliberate use of Savoy imagery in posters and publicity, making the names themselves part of a coherent branding program rather than a loose naming convention.

That branded family resemblance creates an attribution risk today. Similar funnels, livery and advertising style should never substitute for printed ship name, date and route.

The 1930 World Cup Voyage

One of Conte Verde's best-known voyages carried several national football teams, FIFA president Jules Rimet, referees and the World Cup trophy toward Uruguay for the inaugural tournament in 1930. It is a genuine historical connection, but only one episode in a much broader commercial career.

For artifact work, a generic 1930 sailing item should not automatically be described as “World Cup related” without voyage-specific evidence.

Far East and Exchange-Ship Service

After the restructuring of Italian shipping in the 1930s, the ship moved into Lloyd Triestino service and became associated with East Asian routes. In 1942 she served as a prisoner-and-civilian exchange vessel in the carefully negotiated wartime exchange system. U.S. Naval History preserves a photograph of Conte Verde in that role.

Following Italy's September 1943 armistice, her Italian crew scuttled the ship at Shanghai to prevent Japanese seizure. The vessel was later raised under Japanese control. Naval History records her as subsequently sunk in an air raid and the wreck as ultimately scrapped after the war.

Interpretive Notes

Conte Verde moved through South American, Far Eastern, diplomatic and Japanese-controlled phases. Those should remain explicit in cataloging because identical hull identity does not imply identical institutional context.
