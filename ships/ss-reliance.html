SS Reliance

Hamburg America Line · renamed Reliance 1922 · Ship Guide

Overview

SS Reliance had one of the more complicated ownership histories among interwar liners. Launched at J.C. Tecklenborg's Geestemünde yard on 10 February 1914 as Johann Heinrich Burchard for Hamburg America Line, she was provisionally completed during World War I but did not begin a normal passenger career until after the war.

Transferred into Dutch ownership and renamed Limburgia, the ship finally made her maiden passenger voyage in 1920. United American Lines acquired her in 1922 and renamed her Reliance; HAPAG bought her back in 1926 and developed her increasingly as a cruise ship.

A Career Delayed by War

Although physically completed during World War I, the ship's intended HAPAG passenger career never began under her original name. That distinction matters: 1915 is a completion date, not a peacetime service date. The vessel's first normal passenger employment came only after transfer to the Netherlands.

The exact legal timing of the Dutch transfer is described differently across secondary sources, but the operational sequence is clear: Johann Heinrich Burchard → Limburgia → Reliance.

United American and HAPAG Service

As Reliance, the ship worked the Hamburg–New York trade under United American Lines before HAPAG repurchased her in 1926. Her first voyage for the returned HAPAG ownership departed Hamburg on 24 August 1926 via Southampton and Cherbourg for New York.

During the late 1920s and 1930s, cruising became increasingly important. HAPAG used Reliance and Resolute as highly visible cruise ships, generating a rich surviving body of brochures, menus, route maps, and souvenir books.

Fire and End of Career

A major fire on 7 August 1938 gutted Reliance at Hamburg and ended her operating career. Norway Heritage dates scrapping at Bremerhaven to 1940; other secondary works give 1941. Rather than imply certainty where the record varies, this guide treats 1940–41 as the disposal window while identifying 1940 as the stronger dated reference presently available.

Interpretive Notes

Name and flag are essential. Material under the names Limburgia and Reliance should not be merged casually. The same hull passed through German, Dutch, American-linked, Panamanian, and again German registry contexts.

Cruise material is not secondary history. The ship's late career as a cruise vessel was a major commercial identity in its own right and explains why so much illustrated ephemera survives.
