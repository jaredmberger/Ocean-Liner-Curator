SS Canada (1896)

Dominion Line · entered service 1896 · Ship Guide

Overview

SS Canada was a Dominion Line liner built by Harland & Wolff at Belfast for regular Britain–Canada traffic. Launched on 14 May 1896, she began service that October on Liverpool–Quebec–Montreal and also appeared on Boston and Halifax-linked winter routes.

Her history is valuable precisely because it is infrastructural rather than glamorous: migration, seasonal Canadian navigation, mail-era schedules, cargo, and two periods of wartime transport work all mattered more than prestige speed.

A Canadian Migration Liner

GG Archives preserves passenger lists showing Canada in both Canadian and Boston service. That seasonal flexibility is central to understanding the ship: winter ice conditions and changing demand meant the same hull could serve different North Atlantic ports while remaining part of the same Canadian migration system.

War Service

Canada was used as a transport during the South African War and again during the First World War. Wartime photographs and records therefore belong to a military transport context, not simply to ordinary Dominion Line passenger service.

Interpretive Notes

The name Canada was reused by several lines. The safest catalog label is “SS Canada (Dominion Line, 1896)” whenever date or operator ambiguity exists.
