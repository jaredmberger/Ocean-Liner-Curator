RMS Carinthia (1956)

Cunard Line · entered service 1956 · Ship Guide

Overview

RMS Carinthia was the third member of Cunard's four-ship postwar Canadian-service class. Built by John Brown at Clydebank and launched on 14 December 1955, she entered service in 1956 during the final decade in which regular transatlantic liners still carried mass passenger traffic before jet travel transformed the market.

Postwar Atlantic Middle Market

Carinthia reflects a postwar passenger economy in which tourist class was no longer peripheral. Her commercial significance lies in reliable Canadian service and broad-market travel rather than express speed.

Fairsea

Sold to Sitmar in 1968 and rebuilt as Fairsea, the ship crossed a clear line from traditional liner employment into full-time cruising. Later Fair Princess material belongs to yet another phase.

Interpretive Notes

The long cruise afterlife can easily overshadow the original Cunard role. For this project, Canadian-route Cunard material, Sitmar material and Princess-era material remain separate provenance categories.
