The operating layer behind Ocean Liner Curator

Curator★OS

CuratorOS is the connected system of tools that helps keep Ocean Liner Curator healthy, searchable, accurate, observable, and easier to maintain. It works quietly behind the public website—checking the technical foundations, surfacing useful signals, and supporting the research and publishing workflow around it.

Monitoring · Intelligence · Inspection · Publishing support
In plain language: Ocean Liner Curator is the public historical project. CuratorOS is the behind-the-scenes operating layer that helps make sure the project works as intended—and helps identify what needs attention next.

Why CuratorOS Exists

A large research website is more than a collection of pages. Links can break. Search visibility changes. Pages can become slow or inconsistent. New content can be difficult for search engines to discover. A technically healthy site can still have editorial opportunities hiding in its data.

CuratorOS brings those separate concerns into one connected environment. Instead of treating monitoring, search data, page inspection, analytics, and publishing checks as unrelated tasks, it lets them inform one another.

The Tools, Simplified

The individual tools are specialized, but they are designed to work as parts of the same system. A public visitor does not need to know every endpoint or implementation detail; the useful distinction is what each group helps CuratorOS understand.

Monitoring & Reliability

  • Site HealthChecks whether important parts of the site are reachable and behaving normally.
  • Error BusCollects operational errors and status signals in one place.
  • SpeedWatches performance so slowdowns can be identified rather than guessed at.
  • IntegrityChecks whether expected responses, files, and technical foundations remain intact.
  • Link MapExamines how pages connect across the site and helps reveal broken or isolated paths.

Search & Discovery Intelligence

  • Search IntelligenceTurns search-performance data into a clearer picture of how people are finding the project.
  • Curator IndexerHelps track and organize the site’s growing body of pages and structured information.
  • AnalyticsAdds readership and usage signals without changing the editorial mission of the site.
  • Content OpportunityLooks for useful opportunities suggested by existing pages, searches, and gaps in coverage.

Inspection & Publishing

  • Page StudioSupports the development and review of individual pages before or during publication.
  • Page InspectorLets a page be examined quickly for technical and editorial signals from the browsing workflow.
  • Tools IntelligenceBrings signals from multiple CuratorOS tools together so they can be understood in context.

Research Workflow

  • Research CaptureSaves useful source material and observations for later research rather than letting them disappear into open tabs.
  • LaunchProvides a practical starting point for the CuratorOS working environment and its major services.
  • Curator OpsProvides an operational view of the system itself: what is responding, what is stale, and what may need attention.

What Makes It an “OS”

CuratorOS is not an operating system in the traditional computer sense. The name describes its role: it is the operating layer around the project. The important part is not any single dashboard, but the way information moves between tools and becomes actionable.

  • A failure becomes a signal. If a service stops responding, the problem can be surfaced rather than discovered by accident.
  • A metric gains context. Search, analytics, speed, indexing, and page structure are more useful when considered together.
  • A page can be examined as part of a system. CuratorOS looks beyond how a page appears and considers how it performs, connects, and is discovered.
  • Research can move into publication deliberately. Capture and inspection tools help reduce the distance between finding something useful and turning it into durable public reference material.

Public Site, Private Machinery

Most CuratorOS tools are working tools rather than public attractions. Their purpose is to support Ocean Liner Curator—not to make visitors interact with an internal control panel. This page exists to make that machinery understandable without exposing the private operational environment itself.

What visitors see

Ship guides, essays, reference objects, research collections, photographs, source notes, and other public-facing maritime history.

What CuratorOS supports

Uptime, integrity, discovery, technical checks, search understanding, page review, research capture, and the ongoing maintenance behind that public work.

The Point Is the History

CuratorOS is deliberately infrastructure, not the subject of the project. Its value is measured by how well it supports the actual work: keeping Ocean Liner Curator accessible, understandable, maintainable, and useful as a long-term public resource for ocean liner history.

The system can grow as the project grows. New tools can be added when they solve a real problem, and existing tools can share what they know. The aim is not complexity for its own sake, but a more observant and resilient way to care for a digital research project over time.
