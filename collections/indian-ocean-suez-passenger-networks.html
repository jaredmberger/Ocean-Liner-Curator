Networks, not one itinerary

The Suez Canal connected the Mediterranean with the Red Sea and Indian Ocean. P&O services linked ports in Europe with India, East Asia and Australia. Ships, schedules, intermediate calls, passenger classes and cargo arrangements changed across periods. A company route is not evidence that every ship called at every port.

Different destinations and different passengers

Britain–Australia traffic and India–Far East services were related but not interchangeable. Travel intersected with mail transport, commercial freight, colonial administration, migration, and the labor of the people operating these ships. The surviving evidence must determine what can be said about a particular sailing.

War and postwar change

Military requisition and the wartime loss of liners altered civilian service. Subsequent rebuilding, changes in political relationships and increasing air travel reshaped long-distance passenger transport. The destruction of Strathallan should not be projected onto the separate careers of Strathnaver and Strathaird.

Evidence and open questions

For any claimed port call or passage, prefer contemporary schedules and dated records. Distinguish published service patterns from an individual voyage, peacetime from wartime, and original accommodation from later refits. Unknown details should remain unknown.
